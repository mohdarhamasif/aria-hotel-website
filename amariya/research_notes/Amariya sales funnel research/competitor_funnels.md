# Competitor Sales and Landing Pages: AI Receptionist and AI Phone-Answering Vendors (as of October 2026)

Method and date: every vendor page below was fetched on 2026-10-18 (live pages, not archives). Pages were read through a summarising fetch tool, so "verbatim" quotes are as reported by that tool. They are reliable for headlines and button labels, but spot-check them on the live page before quoting them publicly. Unless a line says "verified" or names a third party, every number is the vendor's own marketing claim. None of the customer stats below were independently checked.

Vendors covered: hospitality and restaurants (Slang.ai, Hostie, Loman, Maple, ConverseNow, PolyAI, Popmenu, plus the hotel field via Conduit's list); clinics and medical (Hello Patient, Assort Health, Weave, NexHealth); dental (Arini, Viva); general (Smith.ai, Goodcall, My AI Front Desk / "Frontdesk", Dialzara, Rosie, Ruby, Synthflow); done-for-you and guarantee players (Noem.ai, Trillet, AgentZap, Agentsmith). Emitrr could not be fetched (its AI receptionist URL returned 404) and is listed under Gaps.

## Q1. Page anatomy per vendor: section order, hero, problem framing, how it works, proof, offer, guarantee, CTAs, objections

### Takeaway
Two templates dominate. The first is the "enterprise demo-gate": logos, then features, then integrations, then case studies, then "Book a demo", with no prices (Slang, Hostie, Assort, Arini, ConverseNow, PolyAI, Hello Patient, Synthflow). The second is the "SMB self-serve": hero with a monthly price, free trial, 3-step setup, then a pricing table (Rosie, Dialzara, Goodcall, Frontdesk, Maple). Viva (dental) is the outlier and the closest analogue to Amariya: a dial-the-AI hero CTA, a day-by-day "live in about a week" timeline, an ROI calculator, a comparison table against a human hire, published prices and a 30-day risk-free cancel.

- Hero: "Enterprise-Ready Voice AI Agents for Automated Phone Calls." A named risk-reduction framework, "BELL" (Build, Evaluate, Launch, Learn), sits under the heading "Deploy Voice AI Without the Risks". — [Synthflow](https://synthflow.ai/)
- "Hear Demo" buttons for eight industries link to "#" and play nothing. Compliance badges: SOC 2, HIPAA, PCI DSS, GDPR, ISO 27001, EU and US hosting. Healthcare case: Medbelle (UK and EU), "-30% no-show rates". — [Synthflow](https://synthflow.ai/)

#### Done-for-you and guarantee offers (closest to Amariya's model)
- Noem.ai sells a done-for-you AI Concierge that goes "live in days, not months". Per the search snippet, if it misses one of two results within 30 days of payment, it refunds the one-time setup fee only (not monthly fees, written request required). - Trillet: $49/month, "28-day money-back guarantee — no questions asked", self-serve. AgentZap: 30-day money-back guarantee. Agentsmith: one-time payment with a 14-day money-back guarantee. All three come from search snippets and were not fetched. — [Trillet](https://trillet.ai/receptionist/pricing); [AgentZap](https://agentzap.ai/); [Agentsmith review](https://help.fumotousa.com/en-US/Community/Post/38172)

### Inferences
- Viva's page structure (dial-the-AI hero, day-numbered onboarding, ROI calculator, comparison against a hire, published prices, 30-day exit) is the best-in-class template, and the closest to what Amariya sells. Amariya's 7-day promise matches Viva's "about a week" but is stronger, because Amariya pairs it with a money-back guarantee rather than a cancel-anytime trial.
- Restaurant players compete on setup speed ("same day", "under 24 hours", "24–48 hours"). In restaurants, "live in 7 days" will read as slow unless it is framed as done-for-you quality: tuned, tested and with integrations working. In clinics and dental, 7 days is at or ahead of the norm, and hotel tools quote 4–8 weeks.
- A per-location monthly price ($399–$599 Slang, $349–$1,199 Viva, $150–$350 Maple) is the default anchor. Amariya's one-time licence is unusual. Over 12 months, $2,500 equals about 6 months of Slang Core, and $3,000 is about 3.3 months of Viva Platinum. These are rough comparisons that ignore the minute caps.

### Gaps
- Emitrr: the AI receptionist URL returned 404 and no official pricing was found. Not profiled.
- PolyAI's hospitality page (404), Noem.ai (403) and Popmenu's AI answering page were not fetched directly.
- Hotel-specific vendor landing pages (Canary AI Voice, Conduit, Visito, Akia) were not fetched. Hotel coverage relies on one vendor-authored roundup plus trade press.
- G2 and Capterra listings and archived (Wayback) versions were not consulted, so there is no history of how these pages changed and no independent review scores beyond those shown on vendor pages.
- No UK, EU or Gulf-specific vendor pages were reviewed, apart from PolyAI's data residency line and Synthflow's Medbelle case.

## Q2. Cross-cutting patterns: pricing models, guarantees, proof formats and objection handling

### Takeaway
Pricing splits into hidden (demo-gated enterprise) and published monthly SaaS. No competitor found sells a one-time licence with a bundled minute allowance and a done-for-you, dated go-live guarantee. Risk reversal across the category is weak: free trials and "cancel anytime" are common, while true money-back guarantees appear only among small, mostly self-serve players. Objections are handled unevenly. HIPAA is usually a badge, AI disclosure is split between "we tell callers" and "they won't notice", and almost nobody discusses a fallback when the AI fails.

### Cited Findings
- Hidden pricing, "Book a demo" or "Get a quote": Slang (quote per plan despite "starting at" figures), Hostie, Loman, Arini, Assort, Hello Patient, ConverseNow, PolyAI, Synthflow, Weave. — [Slang pricing](https://www.slang.ai/pricing); [Hostie pricing](https://www.hostie.ai/pricing); [Loman pricing](https://www.loman.ai/pricing); [Arini](https://www.arini.ai/); [Assort](https://www.assorthealth.com/); [Hello Patient](https://www.hellopatient.com/); [ConverseNow](https://conversenow.ai/)
- Published monthly pricing: Dialzara from $29, Rosie from $49, Goodcall from $79, Frontdesk $79, Maple $150/$350 (structured data only), Ruby from $250, Viva $349–$1,199, Slang from $399/location. Per-call pricing: Smith.ai $1.60–$7. — [Dialzara](https://www.dialzara.com/); [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/); [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com/); [Frontdesk](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com/); [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/); [Ruby](https://www.ruby.com/); [Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/); [Slang pricing](https://www.slang.ai/pricing); [Smith.ai](https://www.smith.ai/)
- Anti-per-minute messaging recurs: Goodcall's "No per-minute charges. A bill you can predict." and Loman's "No per minute fees. No hidden costs." — [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com/); [Loman pricing](https://www.loman.ai/pricing)
- Risk reversal found:
  - 7-day free trials: Rosie, Dialzara, Frontdesk.
  - Free forever tier of 25 calls a month: Smith.ai.
  - 30-day risk-free trial with cancel "no questions asked": Viva.
  - Money-back guarantees: 28-day at Trillet, 30-day at AgentZap, 14-day at Agentsmith, and a setup-fee refund if results are missed within 30 days at Noem.ai.
  - No guarantee at Slang, Hostie, Loman, Maple, Arini, Assort, Hello Patient, Weave or Synthflow.

- Urgency and offer devices: Weave's "Lock in 25% off for the rest of 2026, with early access!"; NexHealth's "$50 VISA gift card for demoing in October"; Maple's site-wide automatic discount banner. Hello Patient uses an in-browser voice demo ("Start Talking"). Goodcall offers voice-sample buttons. — sources as above

Dialzara's stat cards rendered with no values. — [Synthflow](https://synthflow.ai/); [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/); [Dialzara](https://www.dialzara.com/)
- Live or aggregate counters: Maple ("Restaurants live right now": 2,190,220 calls), Arini ("1,000,000" booked appointments), Hello Patient ("1.3m conversations"), Rosie ("3.1m" calls), ConverseNow ("2,000,000+ Conversations per Month"). — [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/); [Arini](https://www.arini.ai/); [Hello Patient](https://www.hellopatient.com/); [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/); [ConverseNow](https://conversenow.ai/)
- ROI calculators: Maple (10 missed calls/day × $30 = $9,000/mo), Viva ($23,400 at risk), Weave ("Calculate ROI"), Hostie (an ROI Calculator nav item). — [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/); [Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/); [Weave](https://www.getweave.com/ai-receptionist/); [Hostie](https://www.hostie.ai/)

  - Always disclosed: Goodcall ("Callers are told they're speaking with an AI assistant").
  - Disclosed if asked: Smith.ai ("never claims to be human when a caller asks directly").

- Transfer to humans is mentioned briefly by most vendors (Loman, Rosie "warm or cold", Weave, Smith.ai, Viva "Instant transfer to the right staff member"). Few explain what triggers a transfer. Synthflow names failed attempts or sentiment as triggers. — [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/); [Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/); [Synthflow](https://synthflow.ai/)
- Testimonial quality varies. The strongest are named owners with a city and one blunt outcome (Maple, Loman). The weakest are role-only anonymous quotes (Hello Patient) or headings with no testimonials under them (Dialzara). — [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/); [Loman](https://www.loman.ai/); [Hello Patient](https://www.hellopatient.com/); [Dialzara](https://www.dialzara.com/)

### Inferences
- Amariya's three offer elements (one-time licence, dated money-back go-live guarantee, capped cohort of 30 per niche) are each rare or absent among established vertical players. Combined, they form a distinctive offer. The risk is that "one-time" reads as cheap or unsustainable. The page should explain what the licence covers and how long, and what happens after 500 minutes.
- The apply, then 20-minute call, then invoice funnel matches the enterprise demo-gate, so it will not feel odd. Publishing the price, which demo-gated vendors don't, is a differentiator in itself.
- A "call this number now" line is table stakes in restaurants and dental. Without one, Amariya would look weaker than Hostie, Loman or Viva. Because Amariya is built on ElevenLabs, voice quality is its likely strongest demo asset. Separate demo lines per niche, as Hostie does with "Good Burger", would mirror the best practice.

### Gaps
- Actual contract terms (annual commitments, cancellation) are rarely on the pages. This needs sales calls, G2 reviews or ToS review.
- No vendor was found publishing go-live success rates or refund rates, so guarantee claims can't be benchmarked.
- No independent data was found on how buyers respond to one-time versus monthly pricing in this category.

## Q3. Most persuasive and distinctive pages, clichés, and gaps Amariya could own

### Takeaway
The most persuasive pages let the buyer test the product (dial or text the AI), show dated onboarding steps, quantify the cost of missed calls in the buyer's own numbers, and name real operators. The clichés are "never miss a call", "24/7", "#1", unsourced missed-call statistics, logo walls without stories, and "Book a demo" as the only path. Amariya can own the hard guarantee, the founding cohort, visible price, an honest AI-disclosure stance, and premium-hospitality and aesthetics positioning that no restaurant QSR tool or medical enterprise tool currently speaks to.

### Cited Findings

#### Most persuasive and distinctive pages
- Viva: test-it-yourself in the hero, a day-numbered timeline, an ROI calculator tied to a pricing tier, a comparison against a hire, a 30-day exit, explicit HIPAA/BAA and SOC 2 Type II. — [Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/)
- Slang: the reframing line "Voice AI answers the phone. Superhost answers the guest." plus named hospitality-group logos and a 96% caller-satisfaction testimonial. — [Slang.ai](https://www.slang.ai/)
- Hostie: a fictional demo restaurant you can phone ("Call Good Burger"), a founder story ("Built for restaurants, by restaurants"), CNBC press, and comparison pages against competitors. — [Hostie](https://www.hostie.ai/)
- Maple: a live counter and owner-voice testimonials ("I signed up my second location in like, two weeks"). — [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/)
- Assort: patient-side satisfaction ("4.3/5" from 344K patient ratings) and case cards built as one practice, one metric, one link. — [Assort Health](https://www.assorthealth.com/)
- Rosie: the status-quo framing "three bad options" (yourself, voicemail, answering service). — [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/)
- Goodcall: a transparent build process (Gabi runs Build, Test with practice calls on hard scenarios, then Approve) and an explicit always-disclose policy. — [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com/)
- Weave: an hour-by-hour overnight timeline (5 pm to 9 am) that makes after-hours capture concrete. — [Weave](https://www.getweave.com/ai-receptionist/)

#### Clichés observed (multiple vendors)
- "Never miss a call" and "every call answered" variants: Dialzara ("Who Never Misses A Call"), Rosie ("Rosie always answers"), Smith.ai ("Every call answered."), Goodcall ("answers every call"), Hostie ("Never Miss a Call (or a Guest)"), Maple ("Never Miss Revenue"). — [Dialzara](https://www.dialzara.com/); [Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/); [Smith.ai](https://www.smith.ai/); [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com/); [Hostie](https://www.hostie.ai/); [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/)
- "24/7" in the hero or first fold: Maple, Viva, Dialzara, Weave, Frontdesk. — sources as above
- Unsourced missed-call statistics: Loman ("up to 30% of calls"), Weave ("1in3 calls are missed"), Viva search snippet ("misses 30% of their calls"). — [Loman](https://www.loman.ai/); [Weave](https://www.getweave.com/ai-receptionist/)
- Superlatives: "#1" (Loman), "Top Rated" (Loman badge), "the most natural... in the world" (Slang), "The only..." (Viva). — [Loman](https://www.loman.ai/); [Slang.ai](https://www.slang.ai/); [Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/)
- "Employee" metaphors: "Meet your new best employee" (Loman), "Get My AI Worker" (Frontdesk), "always-on teammate" (Weave). — [Loman](https://www.loman.ai/); [Frontdesk](https://www.myaifrontdesk.com/); [Weave](https://www.getweave.com/ai-receptionist/)
- Logo walls without context, and repeated or duplicated carousels: Slang (carousel repeated twice), Dialzara (about 31 logos, no quotes), Assort (duplicated cards). Arini had placeholder content live. — [Slang.ai](https://www.slang.ai/); [Dialzara](https://www.dialzara.com/); [Assort](https://www.assorthealth.com/); [Arini](https://www.arini.ai/)
- "Book a demo" as the only conversion path, with no price: most enterprise and vertical vendors (see Q2).

#### Gaps in the market (observed absences)
- No dated, money-back go-live guarantee from any established vertical vendor (Slang, Hostie, Loman, Maple, Viva, Arini, Assort, Hello Patient, Weave). Viva comes closest with a 30-day cancel, and small self-serve tools offer generic refunds. — [Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/); [Slang pricing](https://www.slang.ai/pricing); [Loman pricing](https://www.loman.ai/pricing)
- No founding cohort or limited-seat offer observed on any page fetched. Urgency is done through discounts instead (Weave, Maple, NexHealth gift card). — [Weave](https://www.getweave.com/ai-receptionist/); [NexHealth](https://www.nexhealth.com/)
- No one-time licence model among the vertical players. Agentsmith (one-time payment) is a generic, self-serve tool. — [Agentsmith review (search)](https://help.fumotousa.com/en-US/Community/Post/38172)
- Hotels are underserved on the phone channel. Hotel tech is chat or PMS-suite-first (HiJiffy, Quicktext, Revinate), and enterprise voice players quote multi-week rollouts (Conduit 4–8 weeks). Restaurant voice pages speak QSR and casual dining language (orders, POS, drive-thru), not boutique hotel or fine-dining guest experience. — [Conduit blog (vendor-authored)](https://www.conduit.ai/blog/best-ai-receptionist-software-independent-hotels); [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/); [ConverseNow](https://conversenow.ai/)
- Aesthetics clinics have no strong dedicated landing page among the vendors reviewed. Coverage is mainly blogs and directories, and the noted objection is that it "feels too automated for premium clients". — [Stork](https://www.stork.ai/blog/ai-receptionist-for-med-spas-2026)
- Voice quality is asserted rather than demonstrated on many pages (Synthflow's dead demo buttons, Slang with no audio). Almost no page offers side-by-side recordings or real-call excerpts. — [Synthflow](https://synthflow.ai/); [Slang.ai](https://www.slang.ai/)
- Honest AI disclosure is a contested position. Viva and Dialzara sell "they won't know", while Goodcall and Smith.ai disclose. A premium brand stance ("Amariya introduces herself, and guests still prefer her") is unclaimed in hospitality and aesthetics. — [Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/); [Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com/)
- UK, EU and Gulf: only PolyAI (data residency in US, UK, EU and APAC) and Synthflow (EU hosting, GDPR) address non-US data concerns. The SMB and vertical pages are US-centric (US phone numbers, HIPAA only). — [PolyAI](https://poly.ai/); [Synthflow](https://synthflow.ai/)

### Inferences
- Amariya's strongest distinctive funnel would combine the following, none of which competitors offer together:
  - Visible one-time founding price per niche, with a live "x of 30 seats left" counter (authentic only if the counter is real).
  - A dated "live in 7 days or your money back" guarantee shown as a day-by-day timeline, like Viva's but with a refund attached.
  - A dial-the-AI line per niche (a fictional boutique hotel, aesthetics clinic and dental practice, in the style of Hostie's "Good Burger").
  - Recorded call excerpts, given the ElevenLabs voice quality.
  - An honest disclosure stance.
  - A short apply form that sets expectations for the 20-minute call.
- Avoid in the copy: "never miss a call", "24/7" as the headline, "#1/the only" superlatives, unsourced "30% of calls" statistics, "AI employee/worker" metaphors, and logo walls without stories. Before Amariya has customers, replace them with founder-cohort framing, a test-call line, transparent setup steps and a guarantee.
- The 500 minutes a month allowance needs translating into calls (for example, about 250 calls at 2 minutes each, an illustrative assumption only), as Viva does with "95% of offices never exceed their allotment", so it does not read as a cap.
- In clinics and dental, HIPAA/BAA must be explicit and above the fold, as Viva does. A badge alone, as at Hello Patient and Weave, is the weaker pattern.

### Gaps
- No conversion-rate data (A/B tests or benchmarks) for any of these page patterns was found. "Persuasive" is an analyst judgement based on structure and best practice, not measured performance.
- Founding-cohort offers may exist on vendor ad landing pages (for example Viva's fb-landing subdomain, which returned 404) that are not reachable from homepages. Paid-traffic pages were not systematically reviewed.
- Gulf-market (UAE and KSA) English-language competitors were not identified in this pass.
