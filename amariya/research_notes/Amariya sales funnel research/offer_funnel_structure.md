# Offer and funnel structure for a high-ticket B2B "apply, call, invoice" sales page (Amariya founding licence)

Research date: 2026-10-18. Scope: page architecture, offer presentation, scarcity law, guarantees, application vs booking, CTAs, AI-voice legal objections, trust without testimonials.
Evidence labels used below: **[Measured]** = a controlled test, eyetracking study or survey with a stated sample; **[Vendor-measured]** = numbers published by a vendor about its own customers (selection bias likely); **[Law]** = statute, regulation or official guidance; **[Opinion]** = practitioner or vendor advice without data.

## 1. What section architecture works for high-ticket B2B sales pages, and what is the evidence on long-form versus short-form?

### Takeaway
No source gives a universal "best" length. The tests that exist point both ways, and the consistent finding is that length should follow how much the visitor needs to know. A $2,500 to $3,500 one-time purchase of an unfamiliar AI product is information-heavy, which favours a long page, provided the key promise, price signal and primary CTA sit in the first two screenfuls, where most attention goes.

### Cited Findings
- [Measured] NN/g 2018 eyetracking: about 57% of page-viewing time was above the fold (down from 80% in 2010), 74% within the first two screenfuls, 81% within the first three. The remaining ~26% was spread thinly down the rest of the page. — [NN/g, "Scrolling and Attention", T. Fessenden, 15 Apr 2018](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)
- [Opinion, NN/g] Put high-priority content and key business goals at the top, keep major CTAs above the fold, use consistent headers and bold text so scanners find new sections, and avoid "false floors" that make the page look finished. — [NN/g, 2018](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)
- [Measured, vendor-reported] A Marketing Experiments test on a health product found the long sales copy more profitable than the short page across three head-to-head tests. — summarised in [Unbounce, "Size Matters: The Long and Short of Conversion Marketing"](https://unbounce.com/?p=20484)
- [Measured, vendor case study] VWO/Designboost: a shorter home page produced 13% more email sign-ups and 25% more click-throughs to the courses page. — [VWO success story, Designboost](https://vwo.com/success-stories/designboost/)
- [Case study, secondary] Conversion Rate Experts' redesign of the SEOmoz (Moz) Premium page replaced a short page with a much longer one, with testimonials, presentation excerpts and CTAs placed throughout. It is widely credited with about $1M in added revenue. The often-quoted "52% lift" could not be confirmed from a primary source in this research. — [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/?p=20484)
- [Opinion] Unbounce advises longer pages for information-intensive requests, such as paid trials, courses or services, and shorter pages for low-commitment asks. — [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/?p=20484)
- [Opinion] CXL guest post: the long-versus-short debate misses the point, because different products need different pages. — [CXL, "Long form or short form"](https://conversionxl.com/long-form-or-short-form/)
- [Measured, benchmark] Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report (2024 edition): median landing-page conversion of 6.6% across industries, based on 41,000 pages, 464M pageviews and 57M conversions. The 2020 report found SaaS pages had the hardest-to-read copy of all industries analysed, while restaurant and catering pages had the simplest. — [Unbounce Conversion Benchmark Report](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/ecommerce-conversion-rate/); [Unbounce 2020 report](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report-2020/)
- [Measured survey] Gartner (published March 2026; 646 B2B buyers surveyed Aug to Sep 2025): 67% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience, and 45% used AI during a recent purchase. An earlier Gartner release (June 2025, 2024 survey, n=632) gave 61%. Buyers prefer self-service for general information, but they still want seller input on tasks that need context, such as judging whether a product fits their company. — [Gartner press release, 9 Mar 2026](https://gcomdr.pdo.aws.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience); [Gartner press release, 25 Jun 2025](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience)

### Inferences
- Gartner's split (self-serve for information, a human for fit) maps closely onto an apply, then call, then invoice funnel. The page should answer every information question on its own: what Amariya does, the price, what setup involves, the guarantee terms and legal compliance. The call should be positioned as a fit and setup conversation, not as the place where the price is revealed.
- A suggested order, built from the attention data plus the trust and transparency findings below: (1) hero with outcome, niche, price signal and primary CTA; (2) live demo, i.e. "call it now" or an audio sample; (3) the problem, missed calls and their cost; (4) how it works, the mechanism; (5) the offer stack: licence, done-for-you setup, 500 min/mo, what "lifetime" covers; (6) the guarantee, with its conditions written out; (7) compliance and objections (AI disclosure, recording, HIPAA/GDPR); (8) founder and team; (9) the real seat count and what happens after 30; (10) FAQ; (11) repeated CTA and how the application works. Only the first two screenfuls are backed by the NN/g data. The rest of the order is inference.
- Restaurant and clinic buyers are not SaaS buyers. Unbounce's readability finding suggests plain, short sentences, and that matches the user's own preference for brief copy.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found on long- versus short-form pages for B2B offers sold through applications at this price point. The evidence is product-specific and mostly reported by vendors.
- Primary data for the Moz "52%" figure was not found.
- No well-known B2B application-funnel sales page was analysed directly in this pass, so there are no named exemplars.

## 2. How can a one-time founding licence with limited seats be presented honestly? What do the FTC (including the 2022 dark patterns report) and EU consumer law (UCPD, Omnibus) say about scarcity, countdowns and "only X left"?

### Takeaway
Scarcity and deadlines are lawful when they are true. What is prohibited, or treated as deceptive, is false urgency: timers that reset or have no real end date, invented "only X left" counts, and claims of limited availability that are not limited. "First 30 customers per niche" is defensible only if the cap is real, enforced and shown accurately (seats taken versus left), and if it does not quietly reset. Most EU and UK consumer-law blacklists apply to B2C, so for a B2B offer the closer rules are the B2B misleading-advertising regimes and, in the US, FTC Act Section 5, which applies to deception in commerce generally.

### Cited Findings
- [Law/guidance] The FTC staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (September 2022, after an April 2021 workshop) lists false scarcity, such as "only two left" when stock is plentiful or inflated "X people have this in their cart" counts, and false urgency, such as baseless countdown timers, timers that reset or vanish, and "limited time only" claims for offers that are not time-limited, under "design elements that induce false beliefs". — [Venable summary, Sept 2022](https://www.venable.com/insights/blogs/2022/09/the-ftc-brings-more-light-to-dark-patterns-in-new); [WilmerHale, 3 Nov 2022](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/publications/20221103-ftc-renews-focus-on-digital-dark-patterns-with-release-of-new-staff-report); [Hunton, 27 Sept 2022](https://www.huntonprivacyblog.com/2022/09/27/ftc-releases-report-on-dark-patterns/)
- [Law] Under the FTC Act, companies are liable for the net impression created by the combined design elements of their sites. — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/publications/20221103-ftc-renews-focus-on-digital-dark-patterns-with-release-of-new-staff-report)
- [Law] UCPD (Directive 2005/29/EC), Annex I point 7, blacklists falsely stating that a product will be available only for a very limited time, or on particular terms only for a very limited time, in order to elicit an immediate decision. Blacklisted practices are unfair in all circumstances, with no need to prove intent or effect. — [ComplianceGuard, secondary](https://complianceguardhq.com/blog/countdown-timer-ucpd-violation); directive text: [EUR-Lex 2005/29/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj) (not fetched in this session)
- [Guidance, secondary] Countdown timers are not unlawful in themselves. They become a problem when the urgency is false, for example a timer that resets on reload or is tied to the visitor's session instead of a real end date stored on the server. The Commission's UCPD guidance is reported to endorse this reading. — [ComplianceGuard](https://complianceguardhq.com/blog/countdown-timer-ucpd-violation); [CPI TechREG, May 2023](https://www.competitionpolicyinternational.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/TechREG-Dark-Patterns-May-2023.pdf)
- [UK enforcement, secondary] ASA rulings found resetting countdown timers misleading and held that the price must revert when a countdown ends. — [Lexology, "The (almost) final countdown"](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=8625aaee-718c-4360-b818-5749c9bd3fc5)
- [Law] The UCPD covers business-to-consumer practices. Business-to-business advertising in the EU falls under the Misleading and Comparative Advertising Directive 2006/114/EC. — [EUR-Lex 2006/114/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/114/oj) (scope from the directive text; not fetched in this session)

### Inferences
- An honest presentation would show a live seat counter backed by a real database ("12 of 30 hotel licences taken"), state the rule plainly ("The founding price ends when 30 hotels have signed, or on [date], whichever comes first"), and say what happens next ("after that, pricing moves to [the standard model]"). Fake "spots left" numbers and evergreen timers should not be used.
- Small businesses such as restaurants and clinics are often treated like consumers in enforcement. Several EU member states apply unfair-practice law to B2B too, and the FTC acts on deception aimed at small businesses. The practical approach is to treat B2C dark-pattern rules as the floor, even though Amariya sells B2B.
- "Lifetime licence" needs a clear definition: whose lifetime, what is included (software updates? the 500 minutes?), and what happens if Amariya shuts down. This follows the "net impression" principle above and 16 CFR 239's treatment of "lifetime" claims (see Q3).

### Gaps
- The Commission's 2021 UCPD guidance notice and the status of the proposed EU Digital Fairness Act (expected to address dark patterns) were not retrieved, and no primary text was checked.
- No primary FTC report PDF was fetched; the findings rely on law-firm summaries.
- The UK DMCC Act 2024 (CMA direct enforcement) and the B2B-specific Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 were not researched in this pass.

## 3. What is the evidence on money-back guarantees and risk reversal in B2B?

### Takeaway
Academic evidence, mostly from consumer contexts, shows that guarantees raise purchase intent by lowering perceived risk, and that they work better when generous and believable. One B2B study finds that service guarantees raise purchase intent and willingness to pay a premium among industrial buyers. In the US, calling something a "money-back guarantee" means refunding the full price on request, with any conditions disclosed clearly and prominently.

### Cited Findings
- [Measured, B2B] McColl, Truong & La Rocca (2019), *Industrial Marketing Management* 81:78-86: two conjoint studies found that service guarantees increase industrial buyers' purchase intentions and their willingness to pay a price premium, and the authors argue guarantees work as a positioning tool. Effect sizes were not available from the abstract. — [i-cirena summary](https://i-cirena.ubfc.fr/node/11035), DOI 10.1016/j.indmarman.2018.11.015
- [Measured, consumer] *Journal of Service Research* (INFORMS, 2014): more generous guarantees increased patronage intent by making the guarantee more believable, moderated by perceived price and familiarity with the store. — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/serv.2014.0076)
- [Measured, consumer] A 2018 survey (n=210) found money-back guarantees and quality labels reduce perceived risk, which raises purchase intention, with perceived risk mediating the effect. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/journl/hal-02326105.html)
- [Measured, consumer] *South African Journal of Business Management* (2003), an 18-ad experiment: combining service information, price and a guarantee raised buying intent, but the unconditional guarantee added less than the services literature often claims. — [SAJBM](https://sajbm.org/index.php/sajbm/article/download/676/608)
- [Law, US] FTC Guides for the Advertising of Warranties and Guarantees, 16 CFR Part 239: use "Satisfaction Guarantee" or "Money Back Guarantee" only if the full purchase price is refunded on request, and disclose any material conditions or limits clearly and prominently. The Guides also cover "lifetime" claims. — [eCFR 16 CFR 239](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-239); [govinfo 2024 edition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title16-vol1/pdf/CFR-2024-title16-vol1-sec239-4.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Live in 7 days or your money back" is a performance guarantee and should be specific: what "live" means (forwarding active, agent answering real calls on the client's number), when the 7 days start (signed licence plus received onboarding inputs), what client delays pause the clock, refund method and timing, and whether the refund is full. Hidden conditions would conflict with 16 CFR 239.
- Because the research says believability drives the effect, the guarantee section should show how the 7-day setup actually runs (a day-by-day timeline). Note that the memory file mentions a "7-day refund guarantee", while this brief says "live in 7 days or your money back". The two are different promises and the page should use one consistent wording.

### Gaps
- No B2B study was found that measures guarantees for software or AI services specifically, and no effect sizes were available from the McColl et al. abstract.
- No published A/B tests of guarantee badges or copy on B2B pages were found in this pass.

## 4. Application forms or direct calendar booking: qualification, form length, multi-step forms, and measured effects on completion and lead quality

### Takeaway
The strongest data, though vendor-reported, says that letting a qualified applicant book a call immediately after submitting the form raises book rates from about 40% to 75-90%. Field-reduction evidence is older and shows diminishing returns. Multi-step forms often lift completion, but no independent study measures their effect on lead quality. A short application (around 4-6 questions) followed straight away by a calendar for qualified applicants suits this offer best.

### Cited Findings
- [Vendor-measured] Chili Piper customer stories: ChowNow and SaaSOptics went from about 40% to about 90% of inbound demo requests booked; Workato reports 75-80% of inbound leads booking, up from about 40-55%; Apollo saw 50% more inbound meetings booked. Chili Piper also claims an average 92% show rate. — [ChowNow](https://chilipiper.com/customer-story/chownow); [SaaSOptics](https://www.chilipiper.com/customer-story/saasoptics); [Workato](https://chilipiper.com/customer-story/workato); [Apollo](https://www.chilipiper.com/customers/apollo); [Chili Piper Form Concierge](https://chilipiper.com/products/form-concierge-v2)
- [Secondary compilation, from an AI-voice vendor with a commercial interest] The median qualified-to-booked rate is reported at about 62-75% for B2B SaaS, and instant scheduling is reported to raise booking from 30% to 66.7% on Chili Piper data. — [AINORA blog, 2026](https://ainora.lt/blog/b2b-meeting-booking-rate-every-study-2026)
- [Measured, small test] One A/B test with about 1,100 visits per variation: a multi-step form beat the control by 52.9% and a single-step redesign beat it by 40.3% (151 versus 91 extra leads). Lead quality was not measured. — [Conversion Fanatics](https://conversionfanatics.com/?p=499)
- [Unverified claim] "Multi-step forms lift conversion by up to 86%" is widely attributed to HubSpot with no traceable original study. — [Reform blog](https://reform.app/blog/single-page-vs-multi-step-forms-which-converts-better)
- [Measured case] Econsultancy: a form with non-required fields converted 31% more visitors, and the resulting leads were reported as more qualified. — [Econsultancy](https://econsultancy.com/how-to-optimize-your-lead-generation-forms-three-case-studies/)
- [Case, secondary] Expedia reportedly gained about $12M a year by removing an optional "company" field that confused users. This came from internal analytics, not a published experiment. Imaginary Landscape reportedly cut a form from 11 to 4 fields and saw a 120% lift. HubSpot's 2012 data showed a 50% lift going from 4 to 3 fields, but later tests show steeply diminishing returns, often under 5%. That last claim comes from a vendor with a commercial interest. — [FormKeep blog](https://blog.formkeep.com/designing-forms-that-convert/); [Perspective AI](https://getperspective.ai/blog/the-form-conversion-rate-myth-why-optimizing-fields-cant-fix-the-funnel/markdown)
- [Opinion] Measure form variants against downstream metrics such as lead-to-opportunity rate, not submissions alone. — [Pedowitz Group](https://www.pedowitzgroup.com/measure-multi-step-vs.-single-step-form-impact)
- [Guidance, NN/g] Asking for personal data before users get any value is seen as a breach of trust. — [NN/g, "Trustworthy Design", A. Harley, 8 May 2016](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)

### Inferences
- Recommended flow: a short multi-step application. Step 1 covers niche and business name; step 2 covers call volume, opening hours and current phone setup; step 3 covers name, email and phone. Qualified applicants (niche match, seats available) go straight to an embedded calendar on the same screen. Others get an honest "not a fit yet" or a waitlist. This keeps the "apply" step as a filter without losing the instant-booking lift.
- The questions should also feed onboarding (phone system, booking software, languages, opening hours), so the 7-day setup clock can start faster. That supports the guarantee.
- A HubSpot form plus a meeting link handles this, in line with the memory note that HubSpot is the CRM.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed study was found on application-gated versus direct booking for high-ticket B2B, or on how multi-step forms affect lead quality.
- No-show rates for apply-first versus direct-book funnels were not found outside vendor claims.

## 5. CTA copy and placement on long pages

### Takeaway
Attention data supports a primary CTA in the first screenful and CTAs repeated after each major persuasion block, because the lower half of a long page gets little attention. Specific CTA copy tests (first person versus second person, and so on) were not found in reliable sources. The defensible guidance is that the button should say exactly what happens next.

### Cited Findings
- [Measured] Viewing time falls off sharply below the first two screenfuls (74% within them), and the remaining 26% is spread thinly. — [NN/g, 2018](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)
- [Opinion, NN/g] Keep major CTAs above the fold, and use consistent headers so scanners find sections. — [NN/g, 2018](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)
- [Case, secondary] The long-page Moz redesign placed CTAs throughout the page rather than only at the end. — [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/?p=20484)
- [Opinion] Buttons should describe what happens next; generic labels such as "Submit" do not help the user. — [FormKeep](https://blog.formkeep.com/designing-forms-that-convert/)

### Inferences
- Use one consistent action label that says what happens next, for example "Apply for a founding licence" or "Check if your hotel qualifies". A secondary text link could read "Call the demo line first". Repeat the primary CTA after the demo, after the offer stack, after the guarantee and at the end. On mobile, a slim sticky bar with the price signal and CTA may help, but this should be tested.
- Microcopy under the CTA should set expectations for the funnel ("2-minute application → 20-minute call → invoice. No payment today."). This is an inference from the trust and upfront-disclosure findings, not a tested result.

### Gaps
- No reliable A/B evidence was found on first- versus second-person CTA copy, on sticky CTAs, or on how many repeated CTAs to use on long B2B pages.

## 6. Objections to AI voice products and current legal duties: EU AI Act Article 50, US state AI-disclosure laws, call-recording consent, HIPAA, GDPR

### Takeaway
As of October 2026, EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties have applied since 2 August 2026. A voice agent that talks to callers must make clear it is an AI no later than the first interaction, unless that is obvious. The Digital Omnibus delayed the high-risk rules, not Article 50. In the US there is no federal AI-caller disclosure law for inbound calls. California's bot law (SB 1001) and AI-robocall law (AB 2905) are narrower, and the FCC's 2024 ruling covers outbound "artificial voice" calls under the TCPA. Recording calls needs all-party consent in about 11-13 states. US clinics need a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement covering Amariya and its sub-processors, such as the voice platform and LLM provider. Addressing these points visibly on the page doubles as an objection-handling and trust section.

### Cited Findings
- [Law, secondary] Article 50(1): providers must design AI systems that interact directly with people so the people are told they are interacting with AI. No notice is needed where this is obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person. The information must be given clearly and distinguishably no later than the first interaction, in line with accessibility requirements. — [Jones Walker, 16 Jul 2026](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html)
- [Law, secondary] Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026 and the Digital Omnibus did not move it. Violations carry fines of up to €15M or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher, and the lower figure applies to SMEs. The only grace period is for Article 50(2) machine-readable marking by providers whose generative systems were on the market before 2 August 2026, who have until 2 December 2026. — [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html); [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article50-watermarking-deadline/)
- [Law, secondary; sources conflict] The Digital Omnibus on AI moves Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 and Annex I obligations to 2 August 2028. One source says it entered into force on 27 July 2026 as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. Another, dated 16 July 2026, said Official Journal publication was still pending. The Commission reportedly published Article 50 guidelines on 20 July 2026 and a code of practice on marking AI content on 10 June 2026. — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html)
- [Law] California SB 1001 (Bus. & Prof. Code §17940 et seq., in force since 2019) bans using a bot to mislead a person about its artificial identity in order to drive a commercial sale or influence a vote. Disclosure is a safe harbour. — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/blog/i-am-robot-californias-new-law-requires-disclosure-use-bots); [Financier Worldwide](https://www.financierworldwide.com/bot-or-not-navigating-californias-bot-disclosure-law)
- [Law, vendor-sourced] California AB 2905 (signed 20 Sept 2024, Ch. 316, amending Pub. Util. Code §2874) requires automated or robocalls using AI-generated voices to disclose this. It targets automatic dialing devices, i.e. outbound calls. Penalty claims in vendor sources ($500 per call) were not verified. — [Resemble AI](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/robocall-transparency-bill); [Aira compliance guide](https://www.getaira.io/resources/california-ai-voice-disclosure)
- [Law, secondary] A 2026 vendor guide says the US has no federal AI-caller disclosure law. — [AINORA, 2026](https://ainora.lt/blog/ai-caller-disclosure-laws-by-country-2026) (AI-voice vendor; treat with caution)
- [Law] FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17 (8 Feb 2024, CG Docket 23-362, unanimous) says AI-generated or cloned voices are "artificial" voices under the TCPA, so calls using them need prior express consent. It concerns calls placed to consumers, i.e. outbound. — [Mayer Brown, 27 Feb 2024](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/fcc-declares-authority-and-intent-to-regulate-ai-generated-calls-under-the-tcpa); [Wilson Sonsini](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/fcc-rules-ai-generated-voices-are-artificial-under-the-tcpa.html)
- [Law, secondary; sources differ on the list] All-party consent to record is required in about 11-13 states. Commonly listed: California, Delaware, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, Nevada, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, Washington; Connecticut, Michigan and Oregon are disputed or partial. California Penal Code §632 requires consent of all parties to record confidential communications. The usual advice is to apply the strictest rule, e.g. "this call may be recorded". — [Kilpatrick Townsend, Jul 2024](https://ktslaw.com/en/Blog/GlobalPrivacy-and-CybersecurityLaw/2024/7/Wiretap-Laws-in-the-United-States); [Ringio help](https://help.ringio.com/en/articles/6314449-two-party-consent-states-call-recording); [Hostie AI 2025](https://hostie.ai/resources/state-by-state-call-recording-compliance-ai-virtual-hosts-2025)
- [Law, secondary] HIPAA: a vendor that creates, receives, maintains or transmits PHI for a covered entity, for example by transcribing calls about appointments or diagnoses, is a business associate and needs a written BAA under 45 CFR §164.504(e). Subcontractors in the chain (voice, LLM and telephony providers) also need BAA coverage, and a missing subcontractor BAA is a common audit gap. Consumer AI tools do not sign BAAs, and enterprise or API tiers vary by product and configuration. — [Lorikeet](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/ai-support-baa-hipaa-healthcare-guide); [Linford & Co](https://linfordco.com/blog/importance-hipaa-business-associate-agreements); [AI Career Lab 2026](https://theaicareerlab.com/blog/ai-business-associate-agreements-baa-vendor-guide-2026)

### Inferences
- The likely buyer objections are: "Will callers hate talking to a robot?", "Is it legal?", "What happens with patient data?", "What if it gets something wrong?" and "Who owns the recordings?" A "Compliance built in" block can answer these: the agent opens by saying it is an AI assistant (meets Article 50 and SB 1001 without any extra step), a recording notice is played (all-party states), a BAA is available for US clinics if Amariya can actually sign one and its voice platform provider covers its part, a GDPR data processing agreement is available for EU/UK, and calls are handed to a human on request.
- Amariya is built on ElevenLabs. Whether Amariya can offer a BAA depends on ElevenLabs' and any LLM sub-processor's BAA terms. This must be verified before the page claims HIPAA readiness.
- Article 50 applies to the "provider" (likely Amariya, as the party that places the voice agent on the market under its name), and "deployers" (the hotels and clinics) have their own duties for emotion recognition and deepfakes, which are not relevant here. This should be confirmed with counsel.

### Gaps
- No HHS.gov primary guidance on AI voice agents was found. The HIPAA points rest on 45 CFR §164.504(e) as cited by secondary sources.
- GDPR for EU/UK clinics was not researched in this pass: Article 28 processor contracts, Article 9 special-category health data, voice data as personal data, and transfers to US sub-processors. The report writer should not make specific GDPR claims from these notes.
- State laws such as Utah's AI disclosure statute, the Colorado AI Act's timing, and California AB 410 (2025) were not confirmed. AB 410's enactment status is unknown.
- The final Official Journal citation for the Digital Omnibus is unconfirmed, since the sources conflict.

## 7. Building trust without testimonials: live demo, founder story, transparent pricing, guarantees, pilot programmes

### Takeaway
NN/g's trust research names four drivers that do not depend on testimonials: design quality, upfront disclosure (including price), comprehensive and current content, and connection to the rest of the web. Buyer surveys consistently rank transparent pricing as the most-wanted vendor change. With no customers yet, the best substitutes are evidence the visitor can check for themselves: a live demo line they can call, published prices and terms, a specific guarantee, a named founder, and visible compliance details.

### Cited Findings
- [Guidance, from NN/g user research] Four trust factors: (1) design quality, where typos and broken links hurt; (2) upfront disclosure of all information relevant to the customer experience; (3) comprehensive, correct and current content; (4) connection to the rest of the web, since users trust third-party reviews, social media and press more than a company's own claims. — [NN/g, "Trustworthy Design", 8 May 2016](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)
- [Observed in NN/g studies] Users quickly left a service site that did not state its rate ("they don't state the rate here"). Price ranges are acceptable if all line items are visible. Gating content or asking for personal data before showing any value damages trust. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)
- [Survey] TrustRadius (Jan 2025): 45% of B2B tech buyers named pricing transparency as the top change they want from vendors. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/b2b-tech-buyers-crave-pricing-transparency)
- [Survey, vendor] Mixology: 74% of B2B buyers want clear, detailed pricing upfront, and 69% named unclear pricing as a leading frustration. — [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/b2b-pricing-transparency)
- [Survey, older] Clutch (2020): transparent cost information was the single most important website element for 25% of B2B buyers, ahead of case studies. — [IndraStra summary of Clutch survey](https://www.indrastra.com/2020/02/Clutch-s-Survey-B2B-Buyers-Concern-Cost-Transparency-006-02-2020-0044.html)
- [Product description] Wynter offers pricing-page message testing with verified B2B buyers and says pricing pages are typically the second most-visited page on a site. — [Wynter](https://wynter.com/pricing-page-test)
- [Measured, B2B] Guarantees raise purchase intent and willingness to pay among industrial buyers (see Q3). — [McColl et al. 2019 summary](https://i-cirena.ubfc.fr/node/11035)

### Inferences
- A live demo replaces testimonials with a test the visitor runs themselves. Show a phone number per niche ("Call Amariya as a guest of a hotel") above the fold, or near it, plus short recorded sample calls for visitors who won't call. This is the most direct answer to "does it sound human?". No published test of this was found, so it is inference.
- Publish the full price, what is included, the 500 min/mo limit, overage terms and what "lifetime" means on the page. Do not reserve the price for the call. This follows NN/g's upfront-disclosure finding and the pricing-transparency surveys.
- Founder story: a named person with a face, a background (Legit Design Studio) and a reason for building Amariya addresses NN/g's "connected to the rest of the web" factor. Link to LinkedIn, the company registration and the ElevenLabs partnership if one exists.
- Honest framing of having no customers can become the offer itself: "You're one of our first 30. In exchange for the founding price, we ask for feedback and, if you're happy, a case study." This is a common founder-pilot pattern, but no measured source was found for it.
- Third-party proof that exists today: the tech stack (ElevenLabs), compliance documents (DPA, BAA if real), and a status or uptime page.

### Gaps
- No controlled study was found on embedded live demos, interactive voice demos or founder stories as trust signals on sales pages.
- No data was found on how disclosing a "founding customer" pilot framing affects conversion.
- No Wynter survey data on pricing transparency was found, only its product page.
