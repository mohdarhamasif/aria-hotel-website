# Premium, minimal, scroll-animated landing pages for AI and SaaS (2024-2026): examples and techniques for Amariya

Research date: 2026-10-18. Scope: a minimal, near-black, scroll-animated sales page (no 3D world) for Amariya, an AI voice receptionist (particle-sphere orb, Ranade + Supreme, frosted glass pills/cards, floating "ready to talk" dock).

Note on method: gallery listings were fetched directly where possible. Several galleries could not be read: godly.website now 301-redirects to recent.design (http://recent.design/?ref=godly), Lapa Ninja returned HTTP 403, and Land-book did not surface in search. Awwwards was the only gallery that returned dated, scored, tagged entries. Awwwards detail pages list tags and technologies but do not describe scroll patterns in words, so per-site scroll patterns below are limited to what the gallery tags state; anything beyond that is flagged as a gap.

### Inferences
- The recurring winning recipe for AI product pages in 2026 is: one dark neutral plus one warm accent, a preloader or dynamic hero, a single "storytelling" scroll sequence, and an interactive footer. - Webflow + GSAP dominates these winners; Framer appears for the "clean" end (Gather AI, State of AI Design). Lenis is not listed on any of the fetched pages.

## How do voice-AI brands visualise voice and conversation, and which feel premium versus generic?

### Takeaway
Leading voice-AI sites largely avoid literal waveforms in their heroes. The premium end uses either one photographic or editorial image with a single sentence (Sesame) or an instrument-panel visual of what the voice carries (Hume's tone, emotion and timing tracks). The generic end uses a "#1 platform" headline, a logo strip, stat counters, a phone-number form and long feature-card grids (Retell). A working "Start call" demo is the strongest proof device (Vapi).

### Cited Findings
- Sesame (https://www.sesame.com/): the hero is one full-width image (credited "Image by Osamu Yokonami") with the headline "Intelligence with a point of view", one paragraph and a single Download button. There is no orb, waveform, transcript or call button on the homepage. — [Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/)
- Hume (https://www.hume.ai/): headline "Infrastructure to improve voice AI". The hero visual is a multi-track data panel showing tone labels (Warm, Clipped, Urgent), emotion scores (e.g. "Frustration 0.92"), timing gaps and overlap, interruption counts, background-noise SNR, accent, language and intent tags. No audio player or orb. A section reads "There is more to voice than words." There is also a stats bar (50+ languages, 100M+ speech samples, 10+ years). — [Hume](https://www.hume.ai/)
- Sierra (https://sierra.ai/): headline "Better outcomes. Built on Sierra." Conversation is shown as static chat-exchange images and "timeline traces (transcription, supervisor actions, intent classification)", plus about 30 enterprise logos and a trust-badge row (SOC 2, ISO 42001, HIPAA, etc.). No waveforms or call playback. — [Sierra](https://sierra.ai/)
- Vapi (https://vapi.ai/): headline "Voice agents for builders". The demo card has a "Start call" button ("Mic permissions needed"), a 0:00 call timer, an agent selector and use-case tabs (Customer Support, Lead Qualification, Appointment Scheduling). Section "Hear for yourself". Large-numeral stats ("99.9%", "<500ms"). — [Vapi](https://vapi.ai/)
- Retell AI (https://www.retellai.com/): headline "#1 AI Voice Agent Platform for Automating PHONE Calls", subhead "Meet your AI call center from the future". The demo is a form (use case, name, phone number, "Get a call"). There are no audio samples, waveforms or transcripts. Feature-card grids (5, 4 and 7 cards), about 15 FAQs, and stats (~600ms latency, 95.7% workflow accuracy, $0.07-$0.31/min). — [Retell AI](https://www.retellai.com/)
- ElevenLabs Agents (https://elevenlabs.io/agents): headline "Conversational agents that feel human". Voice is shown through a chat-transcript mockup (including a "Request blocked by guardrail" notice), a Voice/Chat toggle, a workflow diagram (triage agent branching into refund and purchase flows) and analytics panels (resolution rate, CSAT). No orb in the page text. — [ElevenLabs Agents](https://elevenlabs.io/agents)
- ElevenLabs publishes an open UI library with an "Orb" component at ui.elevenlabs.io; the docs page returned HTTP 429 and could not be read this session. — [ElevenLabs UI Orb docs](https://ui.elevenlabs.io/docs/components/orb)

### Inferences
- Amariya's particle orb is a defensible brand device, but the evidence points to pairing it with proof rather than decoration. Two options: (a) a Vapi-style live "Start call" or recorded call playback inside the floating dock, and (b) a Hume-style annotated transcript that shows what Amariya noticed (caller intent, booking made, after-hours) as the scroll story.
- Sesame shows that "premium" can mean one image, one sentence, one button. That supports Amariya's generous empty space.
- What reads as generic here is the Retell pattern: superlative headline, logo marquee, stat counters, phone-number form, many identical card grids, long FAQ.
- Transcripts plus outcome tags (booked, transferred, after-hours) are a more concrete proof for a receptionist product than waveforms, which none of the leaders used in the hero.

- PolyAI, Bland and Decagon sites were not fetched (tool-call budget).
- WebFetch reads text only, so motion and visual details (orb behaviour, scroll effects, colours) on these brand sites are unverified; a browser pass is needed.
- No third-party design critique comparing voice-AI brand sites was found.

## Which motion and layout patterns read as "AI-generated" clichés in 2025-2026, and which read as crafted?

### Takeaway
Practitioner catalogues converge on a short list of AI tells: purple/cyan gradients on dark, glassmorphism and glow used as decoration, glowing orbs, gradient text, identical icon-card grids, hero-metric rows, oversized or crushed-tracking hero headlines, badges or labels above headlines, auto-scrolling marquees, fade-in on every element, and scroll-hijacked heroes. Several sit uncomfortably close to Amariya's brand (dark canvas, glass pills, orb), so restraint and purpose are what separate them from the cliché.

### Cited Findings
- The Impeccable "slop" catalogue lists, among others: "Glassmorphism everywhere" (blur, glass and glow as decoration rather than to solve a layering problem); "Lazy 'Cool'" (glass panels, neon edges and glowing orbs pile up); "Radial-gradient background halo" / "Soft spotlight behind content"; "AI color palette" (purple gradients and bright cyan on dark); "Dark mode with glowing accents"; "Gradient text"; "Identical card grids"; "Hero metric layout"; "Nested cards"; "Monotonous spacing"; "Extreme border-radius on cards"; "Badge above the main headline"; "Oversized hero headline"; "Crushed letter spacing"; "Overused font" (Inter); motion tells "Pulsing status dot", "Auto-scrolling marquee", "Bounce or elastic easing", "Animation that changes layout", "Content stuck waiting to appear" (content left at zero opacity if a reveal fails); copy tells "Em-dash overuse" and "Forced contrast" ("Not a feature. A platform."). — [Impeccable: the visible tells of AI design](https://impeccable.style/slop) (one practitioner's taxonomy, tool vendor)
- The purple default is traced to Tailwind UI's indigo; Adam Wathan apologised in August 2025, and the default spread through training data. — [prg.sh: Why your AI keeps building the same purple gradient website](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)
- Lists of AI-site tells include purple gradients, floating particle backgrounds, scroll-hijacked hero sections, and fade-in animations on nearly every element, plus Inter and three-box icon grids. — [Noqta: AI design slop](https://noqta.tn/en/blog/ai-design-slop-overused-ui-patterns-fix-2026); [CodeMySpec: vibe-coded websites look the same](https://codemyspec.com/blog/vibe-coded-websites-look-the-same)
- Template marketplaces confirm the commodity look: dark Next.js/Tailwind/Framer Motion AI templates with a sticky glassmorphism navbar (AuraAI), staggered fade-up feature grids (Inkwell), GSAP + Lenis with on-scroll gradient animation (React Bits Pro "AI SaaS landing"). — [Framer: Inkwell](https://www.framer.com/community/marketplace/templates/inkwell/); [React Bits Pro: AI SaaS landing](https://pro.reactbits.dev/docs/templates/ai-saas-landing)
- Crafted signals from the award winners: a two-colour palette with one accent (Fourmula AI #020108 + #FF6B02; Heron AI #F15534 + #282828), one deliberate "storytelling" scroll sequence, and a custom interactive footer and 404. — [Awwwards: Fourmula AI](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/fourmula-ai); [Awwwards: Heron AI](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/heron-ai)

### Inferences
- Glass in Amariya should solve layering (the dock and nav floating over content), not decorate cards. One glass layer, no glass-on-glass, no neon edge glow.
- The orb should be the single luminous element on the page. Avoid radial halos behind headings and gradient text elsewhere.
- Animate once, per meaningful beat (e.g. a transcript writing itself, a booking confirmed), not fade-up on every block. Content must be visible by default in HTML/CSS, so a failed script never leaves sections at zero opacity.
- Ranade + Supreme already avoid the "Inter everywhere" tell; keep tracking near default rather than crushed.
- Avoid the logo marquee and hero stat row; prefer a single quiet proof line or one real call recording.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or survey data on how visitors perceive "AI-looking" design; all sources are practitioner blogs or vendor catalogues.

## Techniques: CSS scroll-driven animations support, GSAP licensing, Lenis, performance, accessibility

### Takeaway
CSS scroll-driven animations work in Chrome/Edge 115+ and Safari 26+ (about 88% of global users). Firefox support is only at the newest/preview version, so treat it as progressive enhancement. GSAP, including ScrollTrigger and SplitText, is free for commercial use under Webflow's "No Charge" licence (effective Apr 30, 2025); the one restriction is building no-code tools that compete with Webflow. Lenis smooths only wheel input and adds its own rAF loop, so it is optional for a minimal page. Animate only transform and opacity to protect CLS. Gate all non-essential motion behind prefers-reduced-motion (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3, AAA).

### Cited Findings
CSS scroll-driven animations
- caniuse `animation-timeline`: Chrome 115+, Edge 115+, Safari 26.0+, Firefox "supported in 161 only" (not 160 or earlier); 87.84% global support. — [caniuse: animation-timeline](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline)
- MDN marks `animation-timeline` "Limited availability ... not Baseline because it does not work in some of the most widely-used browsers." — [MDN: animation-timeline](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/animation-timeline)
- A Mozilla employee in the Mozilla Connect thread said the feature is behind a preference and still in development; users complained in 2026 that only Firefox lacked it. The comment is undated, which conflicts in timing with caniuse's Firefox 161 entry. — [Mozilla Connect: implement CSS scroll-driven animations](https://connect.mozilla.org/t5/ideas/implement-css-scroll-driven-animations-animation-timeline/idc-p/119840)
- Safari 26 added scroll-driven animations. — [BuildMVPFast: CSS scroll-driven animations go cross-browser](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/css-scroll-driven-animations-replace-js-2026)
- The spec defines Scroll Progress and View Progress timelines. — [Chrome for Developers: scroll-driven animations](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/scroll-driven-animations)
- Chrome's case study (Yuriko Hirota, 2023-07-12): under heavy main-thread JS load, a JS scroll-listener animation became "janky and sluggish" while the CSS scroll-driven version was unaffected. The article gives no numbers. — [Chrome for Developers: scroll-driven animations performance case study](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/scroll-animation-performance-case-study)

GSAP licensing
- The GSAP Standard "No Charge" licence permits use "on any website, web application, or digital interface by any person or entity"; the FAQ says "Commercial usage is covered under the standard license", including formerly members-only plugins such as SplitText and MorphSVG. Prohibited uses: no-code visual animation tools that compete with Webflow; reverse-engineering GSAP to build competitive products. "AI-generated code is not a 'Prohibited Use.'" Effective Apr 30, 2025, last modified May 30, 2025. Webflow may amend the licence. — [GSAP licensing](https://gsap.com/licensing/)
- The GSAP README describes the library as "100% FREE including ALL of the bonus plugins like SplitText, MorphSVG", even for commercial use; install with `npm install gsap`, with no auth token or private registry needed. — [GSAP README on jsDelivr (3.15.0)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/gsap@3.15.0/README.md)
- ScrollTrigger is not named in the licence text itself; the "All of GSAP" wording and the README cover it. — [GSAP licensing](https://gsap.com/licensing/)

Lenis
- Per GreenSock staff, Lenis smooths only mouse-wheel scrolling; scrollbar drag, spacebar and Tab navigation are not smoothed. — [GSAP forum, 2023](https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/36030-use-scrollsmoother-features-with-lenis-scroll/)
- Smoothers that hide the native scrollbar were flagged as an accessibility concern. — [GreenSock forum: ScrollSmoother accessibility, 2022](https://greensock.com/forums/topic/34032-scrollsmoother-accessibility/)
- Lenis keeps the native scrollbar, Cmd/Ctrl+F and CSS `position: sticky` working. — [GSAP forum, 2023](https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/36030-use-scrollsmoother-features-with-lenis-scroll/)
- Lenis runs its own animation loop, which can cause jitter with GSAP or Three.js loops if not synced. Some teams disable it on mobile because it feels unlike native iOS scrolling. — [skills.sh Lenis guide](https://www.skills.sh/avnehsbhatia/ultraui/lenis-smooth-scroll); [svilenkovic Lenis tutorial](https://svilenkovic.com/3d/lenis-smooth-scroll-tutorial) (secondary sources)
- Thoughtworks Radar had a Lenis entry (Oct 2024) that is no longer on the current edition. — [Thoughtworks Radar: Lenis](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/languages-and-frameworks/lenis)

Performance (CLS / INP)
- "Composited animations using `translate` can't impact other elements, and so don't count toward CLS"; changing `top`/`left` causes layout shifts; `box-shadow` and `box-sizing` changes trigger re-layout and paint; good CLS is 0.1 or less at the 75th percentile. — [web.dev: Optimize CLS](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-cls)
- INP "good" threshold is 200 ms or less (standard web.dev guidance; the page was not re-fetched this session). — [web.dev: INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp)

Accessibility
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (Level AAA): "Motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled, unless the animation is essential to the functionality or the information being conveyed." Parallax is "often non-essential". Content moving into view as part of scrolling itself is allowed. Sufficient techniques: C39 (CSS `prefers-reduced-motion`), SCR40 (`prefers-reduced-motion` in JS), or a user setting. SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide covers auto-started animation (relevant to an always-moving orb and to marquees). — [W3C Understanding SC 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)

### Inferences
- Recommended stack for Amariya's minimal page: CSS scroll-driven animations (`view()` timelines, wrapped in `@supports (animation-timeline: view())`) for simple reveals and progress, with static, fully visible fallbacks for Firefox. Use GSAP ScrollTrigger (free) only for the one or two pinned or scrubbed story beats that need precise control. SplitText is now free for word-by-word reveals, but use it on one headline at most.
- Skip Lenis unless the scrubbed story beats feel steppy on Windows wheels. If used, disable it under `prefers-reduced-motion` and on touch devices, and drive it from `gsap.ticker` so there is one loop.
- Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: freeze or slow the orb's particle drift (SC 2.2.2 if it moves on its own for more than 5 seconds), drop pins and scrubs to plain stacked sections, and swap word reveals for instant text.
- Animate only `transform` and `opacity`. Avoid animating `backdrop-filter` blur on the glass dock and cards (paint cost), and reserve space for the dock so it never shifts layout.
- Award winners score lowest on accessibility, so meeting SC 2.3.3 plus keyboard focus on the dock is an inexpensive way for Amariya to look more crafted than the field.

### Gaps
- Firefox's exact shipping status for `animation-timeline` is unclear: caniuse lists support in Firefox 161 only, while the Mozilla Connect thread (undated comment) says it is behind a preference. Firefox 161 release notes were not found.
- Lenis's current official docs were not fetched; the input-coverage claims come from 2022-2023 forum posts and may be out of date.
- No quantitative benchmarks (frame times, INP impact) comparing GSAP ScrollTrigger, CSS scroll timelines and Lenis were found; the Chrome case study is qualitative.
