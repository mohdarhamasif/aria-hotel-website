# Problem evidence: missed calls and front-desk phone load (hotels, restaurants, medical and aesthetic clinics, dental)

Research date: 2026-10-18. Reliability labels used throughout:
- **STRONG**: government statistics, trade body or association survey with a stated sample, peer-reviewed study, or a large independent analyst survey.
- **VENDOR**: produced by a company that sells call-handling, AI or phone software. It can be usable only if it is labelled as vendor data, with its sample and method.
- **WEAK**: no traceable primary source, a figure seen only second-hand, inconsistent within the same source, or circular (blogs citing blogs). Do not print these.
- **OLD**: data from before 2022.

Overall finding: most of the "missed call" statistics that circulate for these verticals are vendor-produced. Trade bodies (AHLA, NRA, ADA HPI, MGMA, AmSpa) publish staffing and revenue data but, as far as I found, do not publish measured answer or miss rates. The safest claims for the page are about staffing pressure (strong sources) and caller behaviour (one older UK study plus one 2025 US vendor survey). Any miss-rate claim needs a "vendor data" label.

## 1. What share of inbound calls go unanswered or to voicemail, and what share arrive after hours or at peak times?

### Takeaway
No independent, peer-reviewed or trade-body figure for answer rates turned up in any of the four verticals. Vendor call-platform data clusters around 30-38% unanswered for dental practices and 23-36% at peak for restaurants. For hotels, the best recent data is a vendor mystery shop (308 calls, 135 luxury and upscale hotels), which measures lead capture, not answer rate.

### Cited Findings
**Dental**
- Peerlogic DSO case study (Feb 2026): 38% of inbound calls went unanswered across a 26-practice group, and new-patient calls converted at 25%. VENDOR (Peerlogic sells phone and AI software). Single customer, not a market sample. — [Peerlogic](https://www.peerlogic.com/post/peerlogic-dso-case-study)
- Dental Economics article "The economics of missed calls": an industry benchmark of 30-38% of incoming calls missed during business hours. I saw this only in a search snippet because the page returned 403, so its underlying source is unverified. Trade-magazine article that likely relies on vendor data, so treat as VENDOR or WEAK until checked. — [Dental Economics](https://dentaleconomics.com/practice/article/55385634/the-economics-of-missed-calls-quantifying-revenue-loss-in-dental-practices)
- "68% of new-patient calls go unanswered; only 42% of answered calls book" is attributed to Peerlogic by VoiceStack, another vendor. It conflicts with Peerlogic's own 38% figure. WEAK, do not use. — [VoiceStack](https://resources.voicestack.com/article/why-missed-calls-are-killing-your-dental-practice)
- "Three out of four missed dental calls happen after hours." This is a share of missed calls, not of all calls. VENDOR (Convirza, call tracking), and no method was shown in the snippet. — [Convirza](https://www.convirza.com/content-hub/missed-dental-calls-after-hours/)
- Claims that 25-40% of all dental calls arrive outside business hours, and that 65% of missed calls are from potential new patients, appear only on vendor or aggregator blogs (Hyperleap, ainora.lt, Twig) that cite each other or unnamed sources. WEAK and circular. — [Hyperleap](https://hyperleap.ai/blog/dental-clinics-lose-patients-slow-response); [ainora.lt](https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-practice-phone-call-statistics-2026)

**Restaurants**
- Maple, "The State of Restaurant Phone Communication" (7 Dec 2025, Aidan Chau). Covers about 1.2M calls to 1,000+ US restaurant and local-business locations, Dec 2023 to Nov 2025. VENDOR.
  - 68% of calls arrive in the lunch (11am-2pm, 28%) and dinner (5-9pm, 40%) rushes. This comes from platform call metadata, which is the more reliable part of the report.
  - Restaurants missed about 33% of incoming calls at peak before adopting Maple. This is merchant self-reported in onboarding surveys, not measured, so it is weaker.
  - Call reasons: reservations 38%, hours/location/directions 22%, menu questions 15%, takeout/delivery orders 12%, events/large parties 7%, modifications 4%, other 2%. Classified by Maple's AI.
  — [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/blog/the-state-of-restaurant-phone-communication-insights-from-1-million-calls)
- Hostie AI ("500k-call study", 2024-25) reports a 36% peak-hour miss rate before its product in one post and 23% in another. The same dataset gives inconsistent figures, so it is WEAK. — [Hostie 36%](https://www.hostie.ai/resources/hostie-ai-500k-call-study-missed-calls-reduction); [Hostie 23%](https://hostie.ai/resources/lunch-rush-case-study-cutting-missed-calls-23-to-3-percent-hostie-ai-500k-dataset)

**Hotels**
- Revinate Hospitality Research Group, 2026 Secret-Shopped Call Report: 308 mystery-shop calls to 135 luxury and upscale US hotels, all of which were non-Revinate properties. VENDOR. I read it via a Hospitality Net transcript; the source page notes that the podcast conversation itself was AI-generated, but the figures come from the report.
  - 92.2% of calls ended with no guest contact details captured. Email was captured on 4.7% of calls and a phone number on 5.4%.
  - 26.3% of after-hours reservation calls went to standard voicemail.
  - Shoppers asked for the booking directly on only 22% of calls. 55.6% of calls had zero discovery questions, rising to 74% when the front desk answered.
  - 14 calls were excluded because they hit voicemail loops or disconnected lines.
  — [Hospitality Net / Revinate](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4134229/we-secret-shopped-300-calls-heres-what-we-found)
- Quality Track International (2007): 25.5% of initial group-sales inquiries went unanswered, and a sales manager was available only 49.4% of the time, across 750+ properties. OLD, and it measures group sales, not reservations. — [HotelExecutive](https://www.hotelexecutive.com/newswire/8359/sales-managers-fail-to-respond-to-customer-inquiries-25-of-the-time)

**Medical practices**
- MGMA Stat poll (9 Dec 2025, n=236 practice leaders): "phone access" was the #3 patient-access priority for 2026 at 22%, behind no-shows (27%) and online scheduling (24%), with wait times at 21%. STRONG, but it measures priorities, not miss rates. — [MGMA](https://mgma.com/mgma-stat/patient-access-priorities-for-2026)
- A vendor white paper hosted on a professional-association site says outpatient call abandonment "frequently exceeds 15-30% (MGMA)". It names no specific MGMA report. WEAK, attribution not verified. — [White paper PDF](https://assets-002.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/pac/WHITE_PAPER_THE-HIDDEN-COST-OF-MISSED-CALLS-LONG-WAIT-TIMES-IN-PATIENT-ACCESS-.pdf)
- CMS benchmark for its own Medicare/Medicaid call-center secret shopping: a passing grade requires average hold under 2 minutes and fewer than 5% of calls disconnected. This is a regulatory standard, not a clinic measurement, but it is useful as a credible "what good looks like". — [Atlas Systems summary of CMS guidance (Sep 2024)](https://atlassystems.com/hubfs/PRIME/Appointment%20Wait%20Time%20Secret%20Shopper%20Survey%20Technical%20Guidance%20-%20Sep%202024.pdf)

**Small business, cross-vertical**
- Moneypenny Small Business Call Report (2016): about a third of surveyed businesses failed to answer incoming calls. Based on a survey of 300 micro businesses plus call data from 10,000 businesses, mainly UK. Moneypenny is an answering service, so VENDOR, OLD, UK. — [Moneypenny](https://moneypenny.com/us/resources/news/small-business-call-report/)

**Aesthetic / med spa**
- I found no source, vendor or otherwise, with a measured miss rate for med spas or aesthetic clinics.

### Inferences
- A defensible page line would be: "Call platforms that track dental practices report roughly a third of calls going unanswered (vendor data)." Avoid a single precise number, and avoid "68%".
- For restaurants, the strongest claim is the timing one (68% of calls land during lunch and dinner service, from Maple's platform data on 1.2M calls), not the miss rate. That timing fits a "your staff are busy exactly when the phone rings" framing without needing a contested miss-rate statistic.
- For hotels, Revinate supports "after-hours calls often go to voicemail (26% in a 2026 mystery shop)" and "most calls capture no guest details". It does not support a claim about unanswered calls.

### Gaps
- No independent, trade-body or peer-reviewed answer-rate data was found for any vertical. AHLA, NRA, ADA HPI and AmSpa do not appear to publish one.
- No credible after-hours share of all calls was found for any vertical. Dental figures (25-40%) are vendor or circular.
- Nothing at all was found for aesthetic clinics.
- The Dental Economics article's underlying method is unverified because the page returned 403.

## 2. How do callers behave when no one answers?

### Takeaway
The most-cited behavioural figures are older (Moneypenny 2016, UK) or second-hand (CallRail 2025, US, n=1,000). Both suggest most callers do not leave a voicemail, and a meaningful minority call a competitor. Widely repeated figures such as "85% never call back" have no primary source.

### Cited Findings
- About 69% of callers who reached voicemail hung up rather than leave a message. Moneypenny 2016, UK. VENDOR, OLD. — [Moneypenny](https://moneypenny.com/us/resources/news/small-business-call-report/)
- CallRail 2025 survey of 1,000 US consumers, reported second-hand; the original report was not located. VENDOR (CallRail sells call tracking), unverified at source.
  - 42% say they leave a voicemail when a business doesn't answer.
  - 21% say they immediately call another business.
  - 78% say they have abandoned a business after an unanswered call.
  — [Beside blog](https://beside.com/blog/missed-calls-cost-small-business); [AnsweringServiceCare](https://answeringservicecare.com/blog/callers-leave-no-voicemail/)
- One aggregator says platform data shows fewer than 3% of callers actually leave a voicemail, attributed to "CallRail 2025; Invoca". That conflicts with the survey's self-reported 42%, which is plausible as a stated-versus-actual gap. WEAK, attribution unclear. — [JustCall voicemail stats](https://justcall.io/blog/voicemail-statistics-2026.html)
- The "85% of callers whose calls are unanswered never call back" statistic has no traceable primary source, according to Beside's own review. WEAK, do not use. — [Beside](https://beside.com/blog/missed-calls-cost-small-business)
- A Klara survey is cited by a vendor as finding 48% of patients say not reaching a human is an "instant turn-off". VENDOR, second-hand, sample not given. — [Commure / related vendor coverage](https://www.commure.com/blog-agents/healthcare-call-center-metrics)
- In Revinate's 2026 hotel mystery shop, 14 of the calls could not be completed because of voicemail loops or disconnected lines. VENDOR. — [Hospitality Net / Revinate](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4134229/we-secret-shopped-300-calls-heres-what-we-found)

### Inferences
- The safest copy is something like "Most callers won't leave a voicemail" with a citation to Moneypenny (69% of UK callers hung up, 2016) and/or CallRail 2025 (only 42% say they leave one). Label both as vendor surveys.
- "One in five callers say they'd immediately call someone else" (CallRail 2025, n=1,000 US) is usable only after verifying the original CallRail release.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed or independent data on how long callers hold before hanging up (hang-up thresholds) in these verticals.
- I could not access CallRail's original 2025 report. Verify it at callrail.com before printing.
- The BrightLocal "75% switch to a competitor" figure (2025) appeared only in aggregator results and was not verified.

## 3. What is a call worth?

### Takeaway
OTA commissions of roughly 15-25% are well established, though the best citation is older. Hotel direct-versus-OTA booking value comes from vendor data. Restaurant and dental value figures are mostly merchant-reported or marketing-derived. AmSpa gives a strong average revenue figure for med spas. I found no credible medical new-patient value.

### Cited Findings
**Hotels**
- OTA commission is "often 15 to 25 percent" (Associated Press, about 2018). OLD but mainstream. — [Fox News / AP](https://www.foxnews.com/travel/are-travelers-losing-the-hotel-booking-wars)
- A 2026 vendor blog lists Booking.com at 15-18% and Expedia at 18-22%, with preferred-partner programmes at 20-25%. VENDOR or blog. — [BookingWhizz](https://bookingwhizz.com/zh/blog/ota-commission-true-cost)
- Phocuswright-based analysis: OTAs drive about 50-72% of online hotel bookings depending on region (US just over 50%, Europe 69%). Booking Holdings and Expedia together account for 93% of OTA bookings in the US and 69% in Europe. Secondary summary of Phocuswright, and the year was not specified, likely about 2018-2022. — [Travel Tech Essentialist](https://traveltechessentialist.substack.com/p/online-hotel-bookings-hotel-direct-vs-ota-cdbd8bfd3f7)
- SiteMinder data on 43.5M reservations: average booking value was US$600 through hotels' own booking engines versus US$340 through third-party channels. VENDOR, and the date was not confirmed. It compares web direct to OTA, not phone. — [SiteMinder](https://www.siteminder.com/news/average-booking-value-direct-hotel-websites-nearly-double-third-party-channels/)
- NAVIS client data: phone reservations average 27% higher ADR and 37.8% higher total guest spend than OTA reservations. VENDOR (NAVIS sells reservation-sales software). I saw this in search results only; the exact source page was not verified. — [Hotel-Online, "Why guests still call"](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/why-guests-still-call-your-voice-reservations-channel)
- Single property (Pride of Britain): average booking value £462 online versus £612 by phone. Anecdotal. — [Hotel Owner](https://www.hotelowner.co.uk/15024-direct-bookings-the-holy-grail/)

**Restaurants**
- Maple: estimated value of a reservation call is $134 (3.2 guests × $42 average check), based on merchant-reported averages. It also gives an "average unanswered call = $85" with no stated method. VENDOR. Use the $134 calculation only if labelled; drop the $85. — [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/blog/the-state-of-restaurant-phone-communication-insights-from-1-million-calls)

**Med spa / aesthetics**
- AmSpa 2024 Medical Spa State of the Industry: average annual revenue per med spa was $1,398,833, up from $1,307,587. Average of 245 patient visits per month; 73% of patients are repeat patients, up from 65% in 2022. STRONG (trade association). — [AmSpa recap](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/)
- $527 average spend per visit, attributed to AmSpa 2024 by a marketing blog. Not confirmed in AmSpa's recap. My own division ($1,398,833 / 12 / 245) gives about $476 per visit. — [Enrich Labs](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/med-spa-marketing-complete-guide-2026)

**Dental**
- $653 annual revenue per patient, attributed to a "2016 ADA report", with a 7-10 year retention giving a lifetime value of $4,570-$6,530. Seen only on dental marketing sites. OLD and WEAK. Consultant lifetime-value estimates range from about $200 to $45,000, which shows how unreliable the figure is. — [Dandy](https://www.meetdandy.com/articles/whats-the-lifetime-value-of-a-dental-patient); [Firegang](https://www.firegang.com/do-you-know-the-lifetime-value-of-your-dental-patients)

**Medical**
- No credible source was found for medical new-patient value.

### Inferences
- The OTA-commission angle is the strongest hotel argument: every booking that moves from an OTA to a direct phone booking avoids roughly 15-25% commission. This is safe to state with the AP citation, noting its date.
- For restaurants, a "worked example" framing (your covers × your average check) is more honest than a quoted per-call value.
- For med spas, AmSpa's 245 visits/month and roughly $1.4M revenue are citable and imply roughly $475 revenue per visit (my calculation, should be labelled as derived).

### Gaps
- No ADA HPI, CDC or MEPS dental spend-per-patient figure was retrieved. MEPS dental expenditure per person would be a credible substitute and should be checked.
- I did not verify NRA or Toast average-check-per-cover data.
- No Phocuswright 2024-2026 direct-versus-OTA share was retrieved.
- No MGMA or other credible figure was found for medical new-patient revenue.

## 4. Staff time on phones, front-desk staffing shortages, turnover and wages (2023-2026)

### Takeaway
This is the best-sourced part of the problem story. AHLA, NRA, ADA HPI, MGMA, BLS and ONS all document persistent staffing pressure, high front-office turnover, and modest receptionist wages. I found no credible measurement of the share of staff time spent on phones.

### Cited Findings
**Hotels (AHLA)**
- AHLA and Hireology survey of 282 hoteliers, run 6 Dec 2024 to 3 Jan 2025. STRONG (trade body; Hireology is co-sponsor):
  - 65% of hotels reported staffing shortages, down from 76% in May 2024. 9% were "severely understaffed", down from 13%.
  - Most-needed roles: housekeeping 38%, front desk 26%, culinary 14%, maintenance 13%.
  - 71% had positions they could not fill, with about 6-7 openings per property on average.
  — [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4125946/65-of-surveyed-us-hotels-report-staffing-shortages)
- AHLA survey of 246 hoteliers, fielded Feb 2026: more than half of hotels were still short-staffed even though 70% had raised pay. Secondary coverage. — [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/hospitality/more-than-half-of-hotels-are-still-short-staffed-despite-70-raising-pay)

**Restaurants (NRA)**
- NRA 2025 State of the Industry: 77% of operators say recruiting and retaining employees remains a significant challenge. I saw this via secondary coverage and should verify it at restaurant.org. — [RestroWorks summary](https://www.restroworks.com/blog/?p=12670); [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-operations/the-restaurant-industry-workforce-shrunk-in-january)
- NRA and Workday understaffing report: 62% of operators said recruiting and retaining was a very or fairly significant challenge. This is a separate survey. — [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/workforce/understaffing-restaurants-costly-likely-get-worse)

**Dental (ADA HPI)**
- HPI Economic Outlook poll, Q1 2025 (738 private-practice dentists, late March 2025): 28.4% had recruited administrative staff in the prior 3 months, compared with 39.0% for dental assistants and 33.1% for hygienists. STRONG. — [ADA News](https://adanews.ada.org/new-dentist/2025/march/staffing-shortages-top-expected-challenges-this-year); [DrBicuspid](https://www.DrBicuspid.com/dental-practice/office-management/staff-shortages/article/15379541/staffing-shortages-remain-reports-ada-poll)
- HPI Q3 2025 (816 dentists): 26.8% had recruited administrative staff in the last 3 months. STRONG. — [Dentaltown summary of ADA report](https://www.dentaltown.com/blog/post/24872/2025-q3-state-of-dental-industry-ada-report)
- HPI late-2024 polling: about 62% of dentists expected staffing shortages to be their top challenge in 2025. — [ADA News](https://adanews.ada.org/new-dentist/2025/march/staffing-shortages-top-expected-challenges-this-year)

**Medical (MGMA)**
- MGMA Stat poll, 26 May 2026 (n=303): 28% of medical group leaders reported higher staff turnover than in 2025, 39% about the same, 30% lower. STRONG. — [MGMA](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/stabilized-but-not-solved-staff-turnover-in-2026)
- MGMA 2025 poll: 29% reported increased turnover. Medical assistants and front-office staff were the most cited turnover hotspots. STRONG. — [MGMA](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/can-staff-turnover-continue-to-be-tamed-in-medical-practices-into-2026)
- MGMA DataDive (2022 data): front-office support staff turnover was 40%, the highest of the support-staff categories. Reported second-hand; verify the original. — [HealthManagement.org](https://healthmanagement.org/pdf/article/staff-turnover-stabilises-but-pressures-persist)

**Wages and supply (BLS, US)**
- Receptionists, BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook. STRONG.
  - Median pay was $38,010 a year ($18.27 an hour) in May 2025.
  - 947,500 jobs in 2025.
  - Employment is projected to decline 2% from 2025 to 2035, with about 105,100 openings a year, mostly replacement of workers who leave.
  - 42% of receptionists work in healthcare and social assistance, and 7% in personal care services.
  — [BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/receptionists.htm)

**UK (ONS, via trade press)**
- Accommodation and food service vacancies:
  - 84,000 in Jan-Mar 2025, down from 107,000 a year earlier. — [Morning Advertiser](https://www.morningadvertiser.co.uk/Article/2025/04/16/job-vacancies-in-hospitality-for-first-quarter-of-2025)
  - 76,000 in Aug-Oct 2025, down 16.5% year on year. — [Parallel Parliament (written answer)](https://parallelparliament.co.uk/question/96018/vacancies)
- Payrolled employment in the sector fell 4.1%, from 2,174,759 (Aug 2024) to 2,085,155 (Aug 2025), the largest annual fall of any industry. — [RSM UK](https://www.rsmuk.com/news/hospitality-vacancies-rise-for-first-time-in-a-year-businesses-adapt-to-new-norm)
- UK vacancies are falling because of cost pressure (employer National Insurance rises), not because demand was met. That is a "fewer people covering the phone" story, not an "unfilled jobs" story.

### Inferences
- A strong, fully sourced page line would be: "65% of US hotels report staffing shortages, and front desk is the second-hardest role to fill (AHLA, 2025)." Another would be: "Front-office staff are among the top turnover hotspots in medical practices (MGMA, 2025)."
- The BLS median of $18.27/hr (about $38k/yr before benefits) gives a credible cost baseline for "one full-time receptionist". A loaded cost would be my own estimate and must be labelled as such.

### Gaps
- No credible study was found on the share of front-desk time spent on phones in any vertical.
- BLS industry-specific receptionist wages (offices of dentists, physicians, traveler accommodation) were not retrieved; the OEWS pages did not load.
- The NRA 77% figure should be confirmed at restaurant.org.

## 5. Evidence on multilingual caller demand

### Takeaway
Census data establishes a large US population with limited English. A 2025 peer-reviewed qualitative mystery-shopper study found Spanish-speaking callers to primary care offices faced more transfers, callbacks and hang-ups. I found no vertical-specific data on the share of calls made in other languages.

### Cited Findings
- US Census Bureau (ACS 2018-2022 5-year, released Dec 2023): 78.3% of people aged 5+ speak only English at home, so about 21.7% speak another language. Of those speakers, 61.0% of Spanish speakers, 48.2% of Chinese speakers and 69.8% of Tagalog speakers speak English "very well". The rest speak it less than very well. STRONG. — [Census Bureau press release](https://cdn.www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023/language-at-home-acs-5-year.html)
- More than 25 million US people have limited English proficiency (ACS 2019-2023), per a third-party summary. Verify against ACS table S1601. — [Boostlingo LEP map](https://boostlingo.com/resources/lep-state-map/)
- Melgoza, Vargas Bustamante et al., "A Qualitative Mystery Shopper Study…" in SSM - Qualitative Research in Health (1 Jun 2025). Calls were made 8-26 Apr 2024 to randomly selected primary care offices in the Los Angeles, Houston and New York metro areas. Spanish-speaking callers were more likely to be transferred, told to call back later, and hung up on, compared with English-speaking callers. Peer-reviewed and qualitative; no rates are given in the summary. — [UCLA Center for Health Policy Research](https://healthpolicy.ucla.edu/our-work/publications/qualitative-mystery-shopper-study)

### Inferences
- The page could honestly say: "About 1 in 5 US residents speaks a language other than English at home (US Census), and Spanish-speaking patients calling clinics face more transfers and hang-ups (UCLA-led study, 2025)."

### Gaps
- No data was found on the share of hotel, restaurant or dental calls placed in a language other than English.
- For international hotels, no data was found on non-native-language guest calls.
- I did not check UK ONS census language data (2021 Census, main language).

## 6. Consumer acceptance of AI voice agents on calls (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
Independent evidence shows significant consumer wariness. Gartner (n=5,728) found 64% would prefer companies not use AI for customer service, mainly out of fear of not reaching a human. Pew found Americans more concerned than excited about AI. Vendor surveys report higher acceptance for routine tasks. The honest message is "AI for routine tasks, with an easy path to a human".

### Cited Findings
- Gartner survey of 5,728 customers (Dec 2023, published 9 Jul 2024). STRONG (independent analyst):
  - 64% would prefer that companies didn't use AI in customer service.
  - 53% would consider switching to a competitor if they found out a company was going to use AI for customer service.
  - The top concern is that AI will make it harder to reach a person, followed by job displacement and wrong answers.
  — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-09-gartner-survey-finds-64-percent-of-customers-would-prefer-that-companies-didnt-use-ai-for-customer-service)
- Pew Research Center, American Trends Panel (June 2025, n=5,023 US adults): 50% say increased AI use in daily life makes them more concerned than excited, 38% equally concerned and excited, 10% more excited. STRONG, though general AI attitudes, not phone service. A secondary source also reports 73% willing to let AI assist with everyday tasks; I did not verify that figure in Pew's topline. — [Pew topline PDF](https://alpha.pewresearch.org/pewresearch-org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/09/PS_2025.9.17_AI-and-its-impact_topline.pdf); [MarketingProfs summary](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2025/53727/Americans-more-concerned-than-excited-about-ai-study-pew)
- Vonage survey (US, Nov 2024): 87% are comfortable with AI support via website chat and 72% via mobile apps, but only 33% during phone calls. 85% say an easy option to reach a live human is very important. VENDOR (Vonage sells communications APIs). — [Vonage PDF](https://s29.q4cdn.com/651905246/files/doc_news/Vonage-Survey-Reveals-Increased-Acceptance-of-AI-in-Customer-Support-with-Nearly-Two-Thirds-of-Consumers-Admitting-More-Efficient-Int-C831M.pdf)
- Telnyx consumer panels (US, Nov 2025, roughly 105-107 respondents each). VENDOR, very small samples, so WEAK:
  - 72% accept voice AI for routine service, and 84% think AI cannot match human reassurance.
  - 60.8% would rather book a home-service appointment via an AI voice agent than fill out a web form or wait for a callback.
  — [Telnyx general](https://telnyx.com/resources/voice-ai-consumer-sentiment-general); [Telnyx home services](https://telnyx.com/resources/voice-ai-home-field-services-consumer-survey)
- Peer-reviewed: a Computers in Human Behavior Reports study (Dec 2024) surveyed 248 telecom customers in Jordan on acceptance of AI voice assistants for support, using an extended UTAUT model. Narrow geography. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/679c17617dc245528d1604fa0b3006aa)
- FHNW bachelor thesis (Switzerland, 2026, n=241 insurance customers, outbound AI calls): nine in ten demand that an AI caller immediately discloses it is an AI. Transparency, control and trust mattered more than usefulness. Student work, so weak authority. — [FHNW](https://studierendenprojekte.wirtschaft.fhnw.ch/view/4968)

### Inferences
- Independent data cuts against a "customers love AI" claim. The credible framing is that an AI receptionist answers instantly, says it is an AI, and hands off to a human easily. That directly addresses Gartner's top concern (reaching a person) and Vonage's 85% "human option" finding.
- The Gartner 64% figure is a risk the page should pre-empt, not hide. Messaging on disclosure and human handoff is supported by Gartner, Vonage and the FHNW thesis.
