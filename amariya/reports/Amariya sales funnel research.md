# Let buyers dial the proof first

Amariya should build its funnel page around three things no established vertical competitor offers together: **a published one-time price, a hard and specific money-back guarantee, and a real, enforced cap of 30 founding seats per niche**. A demo line the visitor can phone carries the proof, since there are no testimonials yet. The competitive field is split between demo-gated enterprise pages that hide prices and offer no guarantee (Slang, Hostie, Loman, Assort, Hello Patient) and cheap self-serve tools sold on monthly plans and free trials (Rosie, Dialzara, Goodcall). The one page that resembles Amariya's model is Viva's dental page. It has a dial-the-AI hero, a day-numbered setup timeline, published prices and a 30-day cancel option, but no money-back promise attached to a go-live date. On evidence, almost every "missed call" statistic in circulation is vendor-produced or untraceable. The page should therefore lead with independently sourced staffing pressure (AHLA, MGMA, ADA HPI, BLS) and print vendor numbers only with a label. Legally, the seat count, any deadline and the guarantee are safe only if they are literally true and their conditions sit next to the claim. Since 2 August 2026, the agent must also introduce itself as an AI to EU callers. On design, the page should be long, calm and dark, with one idea per screen and the price and primary action in the first two screenfuls, where about three quarters of attention goes. Motion should come from CSS scroll-driven timelines plus at most one or two pinned GSAP story beats, all of which must switch off under reduced motion.

## Competitors hide the price and promise nothing, which leaves Amariya an opening

Two page templates dominate the AI receptionist category. The first is the **enterprise demo-gate**: logos, then features, then integrations, then case studies, then "Book a demo", with no price. Slang uses it for restaurants: "Starting at $399 per location", but every plan's button says "Get a quote", and the page states no guarantee ([Slang pricing](https://www.slang.ai/pricing)). Hostie and Loman show no prices at all ([Hostie pricing](https://www.hostie.ai/pricing); [Loman pricing](https://www.loman.ai/pricing)), and neither do Assort or Hello Patient in medical ([Assort Health](https://www.assorthealth.com/); [Hello Patient](https://www.hellopatient.com/)). The second is the **SMB self-serve** template: a monthly price in the hero, a free trial, three setup steps and a pricing table. Rosie starts at $49 a month with a 7-day free trial ([Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/)), Dialzara at $29 ([Dialzara](https://www.dialzara.com/)), and Goodcall at $79 ([Goodcall](https://www.goodcall.com/)).

**Viva's dental page is the closest analogue to Amariya and the strongest page in the set** ([Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/)). Its hero carries a "Call the AI" phone number next to "Book a Demo". Setup is a day-numbered timeline, from "Days 1–2, Connect your PMS" to "Day 7, Let the phones ring", and ends with a Day 30 review where you can "Cancel — no questions asked". An ROI calculator ties into a pricing tier. A comparison table sets Viva against a front-desk hire ("$3,800+ loaded") and answering services. Prices are published at $349, $899 and $1,199 a month. HIPAA/BAA and SOC 2 Type II badges sit above the fold. The table below sets this against the other reference points the buyer is likely to have seen.

The gaps line up with Amariya's offer almost exactly. **No established vertical vendor offers a dated money-back go-live guarantee.** True money-back guarantees appear only among small self-serve tools such as Trillet (28 days) and AgentZap (30 days), and those terms come from search snippets rather than fetched pages ([Trillet](https://trillet.ai/receptionist/pricing); [AgentZap](https://agentzap.ai/)). **No page fetched used a founding cohort or limited seats.** Urgency is bought with discounts instead, such as Weave's "Lock in 25% off for the rest of 2026" and NexHealth's $50 gift card for taking a demo ([Weave](https://www.getweave.com/ai-receptionist/); [NexHealth](https://www.nexhealth.com/)). **No vertical player sells a one-time licence.** Hotels are underserved on the phone channel: hotel tech is chat-first or bundled into PMS suites, and Conduit quotes 4–8 week rollouts ([Conduit, vendor-authored roundup](https://www.conduit.ai/blog/best-ai-receptionist-software-independent-hotels)). Restaurant voice pages talk about QSR orders and drive-thrus rather than fine-dining guest experience ([ConverseNow](https://conversenow.ai/); [Maple](https://www.maple.inc/)). Aesthetics clinics have no strong dedicated page. The objection raised in reviews is that an AI front desk "feels too automated for premium clients" ([Stork](https://www.stork.ai/blog/ai-receptionist-for-med-spas-2026)). That objection is the one Amariya's premium voice and design should answer.

The best ideas to borrow are specific. Slang's reframing line, "Voice AI answers the phone. Superhost answers the guest", is the most memorable sentence in the category ([Slang.ai](https://www.slang.ai/)). Hostie lets prospects phone a fictional restaurant, "Good Burger", which suggests one fictional boutique hotel, aesthetics clinic and dental practice for Amariya ([Hostie](https://www.hostie.ai/)). Weave tells the after-hours story as an hour-by-hour timeline from 5 pm to 9 am ([Weave](https://www.getweave.com/ai-receptionist/)). Rosie frames the status quo as "three bad options": answer every call yourself, send it to voicemail, or pay an answering service ([Rosie](https://heyrosie.com/)). Assort shows patient-side satisfaction ("4.3/5" across 344K ratings), which answers "will callers hate it?" ([Assort Health](https://www.assorthealth.com/)). The clichés are just as clear and should be banned from the copy: "never miss a call" (used by Dialzara, Rosie, Smith.ai, Goodcall, Hostie and Maple), "24/7" in the hero, "#1" and "the only" superlatives, "AI employee" metaphors, and logo walls without stories. Some competitors also publish broken proof: Synthflow's "Hear Demo" buttons link to "#", Dialzara's stat cards render empty, and Arini had "Placeholder" labels live ([Synthflow](https://synthflow.ai/); [Dialzara](https://www.dialzara.com/); [Arini](https://www.arini.ai/)). For a page with no customers yet, a single dead link costs more than it does for them.

One positioning risk deserves a direct answer on the page. Restaurant vendors compete on setup speed ("same day", "under 24 hours"), so a seven-day promise reads as slow there unless it is framed as done-for-you quality: tuned, tested on hard scenarios, with integrations working. In clinics and dental, seven days matches or beats the norm, and in hotels it is far ahead of 4–8 weeks. The one-time licence carries a second risk, because it can read as cheap or unsustainable. The page must say what the licence covers and for how long, and what happens after 500 minutes in a month.

## Staffing data is solid, but most missed-call statistics fail an honesty check

The research found **no independent, trade-body or peer-reviewed measurement of call answer rates** in any of the four verticals. AHLA, NRA, ADA HPI, MGMA and AmSpa publish staffing and revenue data, not miss rates. That changes the problem section. The honest story is about **staff stretched thin when the phone rings**, backed by strong sources, with vendor call data as labelled colour rather than the headline.

The strongly sourced lines are these. **65% of US hotels reported staffing shortages, and front desk was the second most-needed role at 26%**, according to the AHLA and Hireology survey of 282 hoteliers ([Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4125946/65-of-surveyed-us-hotels-report-staffing-shortages)). A February 2026 AHLA survey found more than half of hotels still short-staffed even though 70% had raised pay ([MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/hospitality/more-than-half-of-hotels-are-still-short-staffed-despite-70-raising-pay)). In medical groups, **front-office staff and medical assistants were the most-cited turnover hotspots** in MGMA's 2025 poll ([MGMA](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/can-staff-turnover-continue-to-be-tamed-in-medical-practices-into-2026)), and 28% of leaders reported higher turnover in May 2026 than in 2025 ([MGMA](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/stabilized-but-not-solved-staff-turnover-in-2026)). In dental, about 62% of dentists expected staffing shortages to be their top challenge for 2025 ([ADA News](https://adanews.ada.org/new-dentist/2025/march/staffing-shortages-top-expected-challenges-this-year)). The BLS puts the **median receptionist wage at $38,010 a year ($18.27 an hour) in May 2025**, before benefits ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/receptionists.htm)). That figure is the honest baseline for any comparison with a hire. By my own arithmetic, the $2,500 hospitality licence is about 6.6% of one median receptionist's annual pay, but Amariya should present that as a derived figure and not as a like-for-like substitution. On multilingual demand, **about 21.7% of US residents aged 5 and over speak a language other than English at home** ([US Census Bureau](https://cdn.www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023/language-at-home-acs-5-year.html)). A 2025 peer-reviewed mystery-shopper study found Spanish-speaking callers to primary care offices were more often transferred, told to call back, or hung up on ([UCLA Center for Health Policy Research](https://healthpolicy.ucla.edu/our-work/publications/qualitative-mystery-shopper-study)).

Vendor numbers can be printed only with a label, and some are better than others. For restaurants, the most reliable vendor figure is about timing, not misses. Maple's platform data on about 1.2M calls shows **68% of calls arriving during the lunch and dinner rushes**, exactly when staff are busiest. Its "33% missed at peak" figure is merchant self-reported and weaker ([Maple](https://www.maple.inc/blog/the-state-of-restaurant-phone-communication-insights-from-1-million-calls)). For hotels, Revinate's 2026 mystery shop of 308 calls to 135 luxury and upscale properties found **26.3% of after-hours reservation calls went to standard voicemail and 92.2% of calls captured no guest contact details** ([Hospitality Net / Revinate](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4134229/we-secret-shopped-300-calls-heres-what-we-found)). That supports a lead-capture argument, not an "unanswered calls" one. The hotel economics argument is OTA commission, "often 15 to 25 percent" per the Associated Press. This is mainstream but dated to about 2018, so print it with the year ([Fox News / AP](https://www.foxnews.com/travel/are-travelers-losing-the-hotel-booking-wars)). For dental, a single Peerlogic customer case shows 38% of calls unanswered across 26 practices, and Dental Economics cites a 30–38% benchmark that could not be verified at source ([Peerlogic](https://www.peerlogic.com/post/peerlogic-dso-case-study); [Dental Economics](https://dentaleconomics.com/practice/article/55385634/the-economics-of-missed-calls-quantifying-revenue-loss-in-dental-practices)). The defensible wording is "call platforms report roughly a third of dental calls going unanswered (vendor data)", with no precise figure. For aesthetics, AmSpa's trade data gives **$1,398,833 average annual revenue and 245 patient visits a month per med spa**, which implies roughly $476 per visit by my division ([AmSpa](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/2024-medical-spa-state-of-the-industry-executive-report-recap/)). That is enough for a worked example, but it must be labelled as derived.

On caller behaviour, Moneypenny's 2016 UK data found **about 69% of callers who reached voicemail hung up without leaving a message**. It is vendor data and old, so print it with both labels ([Moneypenny](https://moneypenny.com/us/resources/news/small-business-call-report/)). CallRail's 2025 survey of 1,000 US consumers, in which 21% said they would immediately call another business, reached this research only second-hand, so verify it before printing ([Beside](https://beside.com/blog/missed-calls-cost-small-business)).

## Price, seats and guarantee persuade only when they are literally true

**Publish the full price on the page and keep it out of the call.** Users in NN/g's trust research abandoned service sites that did not state their rate ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). In TrustRadius's January 2025 survey, 45% of B2B tech buyers named pricing transparency as the change they most want from vendors ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/b2b-tech-buyers-crave-pricing-transparency)). Gartner's March 2026 survey found 67% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience for information but still want a seller to judge fit ([Gartner](https://gcomdr.pdo.aws.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience)). That maps directly onto apply, then call, then invoice. The page answers every information question (price, inclusions, setup, guarantee terms, compliance), and the 20-minute call is presented as a fit-and-setup conversation, not a price reveal. Visible price is a differentiator in its own right against the demo-gated field.

The price needs an anchor that the buyer already uses. Monthly per-location pricing is the category default, so a rough comparison helps. By my arithmetic, $2,500 equals about 6.3 months of Slang Core at $399 ([Slang pricing](https://www.slang.ai/pricing)), $3,000 equals about 3.3 months of Viva Platinum at $899 ([Viva](https://www.getviva.ai/)), and $3,500 equals about 7.8 months of Twig's med-spa plan at $449 ([Twig](https://www.twig.so/industries/medspa)). These ignore differences in minute caps and should be footnoted as approximate. The 500 minutes a month also need translating into calls so the allowance does not read as a cap. Viva does this with "95% of offices never exceed their allotment". An illustrative "about 250 two-minute calls" works only if it is labelled as an assumption, and the overage rule must sit next to it. Finally, define "lifetime" precisely: the lifetime of what (the licence, the business, Amariya the company), whether updates are included, whether the 500 minutes continue indefinitely, and what happens if Amariya shuts down. The FTC judges the net impression of a page, and 16 CFR 239 explicitly covers "lifetime" claims ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/publications/20221103-ftc-renews-focus-on-digital-dark-patterns-with-release-of-new-staff-report); [eCFR 16 CFR 239](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-239)).

**Scarcity is lawful only when it is real.** The FTC's 2022 staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" names false scarcity ("only two left" when stock is plentiful) and false urgency (countdowns that reset, "limited time" offers that are not limited) as design elements that induce false beliefs ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/blogs/2022/09/the-ftc-brings-more-light-to-dark-patterns-in-new); [Hunton](https://www.huntonprivacyblog.com/2022/09/27/ftc-releases-report-on-dark-patterns/)). In the EU, Annex I point 7 of the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive blacklists falsely claiming an offer is available only for a very limited time ([EUR-Lex 2005/29/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj)). The UK ASA has ruled resetting countdown timers misleading and held that the price must actually revert when the timer ends ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=8625aaee-718c-4360-b818-5749c9bd3fc5)). The UCPD formally covers B2C, and B2B advertising falls under Directive 2006/114/EC ([EUR-Lex 2006/114/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/114/oj)). But restaurants and small clinics are often treated like consumers in practice, so the B2C rules are the right floor. In practice this means a **seat counter driven by a real database** ("12 of 30 hotel licences taken"), the rule stated in words ("The founding price ends when 30 hotels have signed"), and what comes after ("then pricing moves to [standard model]"). No evergreen timer and no invented "spots left" number. If a date is added, it must be a server-side date and the price must actually change when it passes. On Mobbin, Oevra shows the restrained version: "One time payment / Only 200 available", with no timer ([Mobbin: Oevra](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/1075f05e-7bfc-45ac-96b7-e583baf6bbf8)). Sunday's "Join the Founding Family / Apply" treats "invite-only" as a plain fact ([Mobbin: Sunday](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/ada6310f-fa68-4870-82d6-3ca2b97e267a)).

**The guarantee must mean a full refund with its conditions written beside it.** Under the FTC's guides, "Money Back Guarantee" may be used only if the full purchase price is refunded on request, with any material conditions disclosed clearly and prominently ([eCFR 16 CFR 239](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-239)). The research supports making it generous and specific. McColl, Truong and La Rocca's conjoint studies found **service guarantees raise industrial buyers' purchase intent and willingness to pay a premium** ([Industrial Marketing Management summary](https://i-cirena.ubfc.fr/node/11035)), and consumer research finds that more generous guarantees work by being more believable ([Journal of Service Research](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/serv.2014.0076)). One wording conflict must be settled before anything is designed. Amariya's internal offer note describes a "7-day refund guarantee", while the funnel brief frames it as "live in 7 days or your money back". These are different promises: one is a satisfaction window, the other a performance deadline, and the page must carry exactly one of them. If it is the go-live version, the terms block should define "live" (forwarding active and the agent answering real calls on the client's own number), say when the clock starts (signed licence plus received onboarding inputs), list which client-side delays pause it, and state the refund method and timing. Showing the seven days as a dated timeline, as Viva does, makes the promise believable. On Mobbin, the strongest precedent puts the guarantee next to the price rather than in its own section: Agentcard sets a "30-day money-back" badge inline beside the number ([Mobbin: Agentcard](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/bc4c8057-62e6-48d0-9ad7-e0930c58ce74)), and Surfshark and Superpower put it directly under the button.

**Compliance doubles as objection handling.** **EU AI Act Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026.** An AI system that talks to people must tell them so no later than the first interaction, unless that is obvious, with fines up to €15M or 3% of turnover. The Digital Omnibus delayed the high-risk rules but not Article 50 ([Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html)). In California, SB 1001 bans bots that mislead people about their artificial identity to drive a sale, and disclosure is the safe harbour ([Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/blog/i-am-robot-californias-new-law-requires-disclosure-use-bots)). The FCC's 2024 ruling and California's AB 2905 target outbound AI calls, not inbound reception ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/02/fcc-declares-authority-and-intent-to-regulate-ai-generated-calls-under-the-tcpa)). About 11–13 US states require all-party consent to record, so a recording notice on every call is the safe default ([Kilpatrick Townsend](https://ktslaw.com/en/Blog/GlobalPrivacy-and-CybersecurityLaw/2024/7/Wiretap-Laws-in-the-United-States)). For US clinics and dental practices, a vendor that handles PHI is a business associate and needs a written BAA under 45 CFR §164.504(e). The BAA chain must also cover subcontractors such as the voice, LLM and telephony providers ([Lorikeet](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/ai-support-baa-hipaa-healthcare-guide)). **Amariya must confirm that ElevenLabs and any LLM sub-processor will sign BAAs before the page claims HIPAA readiness.** Viva puts "HIPAA Compliant · BAA Signed" above the fold, which raises the bar in dental. GDPR terms for EU and UK clinics were not researched, so the page should make no specific GDPR claim until counsel has reviewed it. A "Compliance built in" block can answer five objections at once: she says she is an AI, a recording notice plays, a person takes over on request, a BAA is available (once verified), and the client owns its recordings.

**The application should filter without adding friction.** The best data available, all vendor-reported, says that letting a qualified applicant book a call immediately after the form raises booking from about 40% to 75–90%. ChowNow and SaaSOptics both went from about 40% to about 90% ([Chili Piper: ChowNow](https://chilipiper.com/customer-story/chownow); [Chili Piper: SaaSOptics](https://www.chilipiper.com/customer-story/saasoptics)). One A/B test found a multi-step form beat its control by 52.9%, though lead quality was not measured ([Conversion Fanatics](https://conversionfanatics.com/?p=499)). The practical design is a three-step application: niche and business name; then call volume, hours and current phone setup; then contact details. Qualified applicants in a niche with open seats then see a HubSpot calendar on the same screen. The questions should also collect onboarding inputs (phone system, booking software, languages), which shortens the seven-day clock and protects the guarantee. Every button should say what happens next, for example "Apply for a founding licence", with microcopy underneath such as "2-minute application, 20-minute call, then invoice. No payment today." Cloudstudio's small "How it starts" row ("30-min call → proposal in 5 days / no commitment") is the Mobbin precedent for that line ([Mobbin: Cloudstudio](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/a388304a-64f5-4a10-ae21-ac9b93f243cb)).

## One idea per screen, with the price and the demo inside the first two

Attention data sets the page's spine. In NN/g's 2018 eyetracking, **57% of viewing time went above the fold, 74% to the first two screenfuls and 81% to the first three**, with the remaining quarter spread thinly below ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). Long-versus-short tests point both ways ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/?p=20484); [VWO](https://vwo.com/success-stories/designboost/)). The consistent rule is that length follows how much the buyer needs to know, and a $2,500–$3,500 purchase of an unfamiliar AI product needs a lot. So the page should be long, but the hero must already carry the outcome, the niche, a price signal, the demo and the one primary action. The primary CTA then repeats after the demo, after the offer, after the guarantee and at the close. The section order below combines that attention data with the patterns that recur across the Mobbin references. Only the first two rows are directly supported by measurement; the rest of the order is inference.

The demo is the testimonial substitute, so it must work every time. Separate lines per niche, modelled on Hostie's fictional restaurant, let a hotel owner phone a hotel and a dentist phone a practice. Short recorded excerpts serve visitors who will not call. Among voice-AI brands, the premium end avoids literal waveforms. Sesame's hero is one image, one sentence and one button ([Sesame](https://www.sesame.com/)), and Hume shows what the voice carries, such as tone labels and timing gaps, as an annotated data panel ([Hume](https://www.hume.ai/)). The generic end is Retell's "#1" headline, logo strip, stat counters and grids of feature cards ([Retell AI](https://www.retellai.com/)). That points Amariya toward a **timestamped transcript with outcome tags** (booked, transferred, after-hours) as the main proof visual, with the orb as the voice. Only SpaceXAI on Mobbin pairs an orb with a speech bubble showing the agent's line, so that idea is untested but on-brand ([Mobbin: SpaceXAI](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/321998de-d085-41a9-a84b-83ffa1ee9493)). Every Mobbin hero with a demo also carries a customer logo strip. Amariya has no customers, so it should leave that space empty rather than fake one.

Several items on practitioner lists of "AI-generated" design tells sit close to Amariya's own brand: dark canvas, glass panels, glowing orbs. Restraint is what separates the brand from the cliché. The Impeccable catalogue flags glassmorphism used as decoration, radial halos behind content, purple-and-cyan on dark, gradient text, identical icon-card grids, hero metric rows, badges above headlines, auto-scrolling marquees, bounce easing, and content left at zero opacity when a reveal fails ([Impeccable](https://impeccable.style/slop)). Other lists add fade-in on every element and scroll-hijacked heroes ([Noqta](https://noqta.tn/en/blog/ai-design-slop-overused-ui-patterns-fix-2026)). The 2026 Awwwards winners in AI show the crafted alternative: **one near-black plus one accent**, such as Fourmula AI's #020108 with orange, one deliberate storytelling sequence, and a custom footer ([Awwwards: Fourmula AI](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/fourmula-ai); [Awwwards: Heron AI](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/heron-ai)). The design-system digest drawn from the local design-md library (ElevenLabs, Linear, Apple, Resend entries) turns this into rules for Amariya. Keep the near-black #050506 canvas and use a **mint accent only for focus, live states and the active pill**, with blue and orange confined to the orb and at most one glow per section. Glass solves layering for the dock and nav and never nests. The off-white CTA pill is the brightest pixel on screen, and the featured licence lifts by a surface step rather than colour. Shadows sit only under floating glass and the orb, and prices, times and seat counts use tabular numerals. Section openings should vary, because eyebrow, then H2, then lead paragraph is the most recognisable template pattern of all. These notes assumed the current near-black live baseline. If the per-niche 3D worlds or light themes from the redesign direction go ahead, the same discipline of one accent, one glow and one bright action carries over unchanged.

## CSS timelines for reveals, GSAP for one pinned story beat

**CSS scroll-driven animations now cover about 88% of global users**: Chrome and Edge 115+, and Safari 26+ ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline)). They still are not Baseline, and Firefox's status is unclear: caniuse lists support in version 161 only, while a Mozilla thread says the feature is still behind a preference ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/animation-timeline); [Mozilla Connect](https://connect.mozilla.org/t5/ideas/implement-css-scroll-driven-animations-animation-timeline/idc-p/119840)). They run off the main thread. In Chrome's case study, a JS scroll-listener animation turned "janky and sluggish" under main-thread load while the CSS version was unaffected ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/scroll-animation-performance-case-study)). Use them for simple reveals and progress indicators, using `view()` timelines inside `@supports (animation-timeline: view())`, with content fully visible by default so Firefox and any failed script show a finished static page.

Reserve **GSAP ScrollTrigger** for the one or two beats that need pinning and scrubbing. GSAP, including ScrollTrigger and SplitText, has been free for commercial use since Webflow's "No Charge" licence took effect on 30 April 2025. The only prohibited use is building no-code tools that compete with Webflow ([GSAP licensing](https://gsap.com/licensing/)). The beats worth pinning are the ones that carry proof. The first is a **call transcript that writes itself line by line** and ends with an outcome tag such as "Booked: Thursday 7:30 pm, table for four". The second is the **seven-day setup stepper**, with each day lighting as the reader scrolls, which turns the guarantee into a visible process. Weave's 5 pm-to-9 am overnight timeline suggests a third option for hotels: an after-hours sequence in which the clock advances as calls are handled. Use SplitText word reveals on one headline at most. Skip Lenis unless scrubbed beats feel steppy on Windows mouse wheels. It smooths only wheel input and adds its own animation loop, which can jitter against GSAP or the Three.js orb unless driven from `gsap.ticker`, and many teams disable it on touch devices ([GSAP forum](https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/36030-use-scrollsmoother-features-with-lenis-scroll/)).

Performance and accessibility rules decide whether the motion reads as crafted. Animate only `transform` and `opacity`, since composited `translate` changes do not count toward CLS while `top`, `left` and `box-shadow` changes do ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-cls)). Never animate `backdrop-filter` on the glass dock, and reserve the dock's space so it never shifts layout. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, pins and scrubs collapse to plain stacked sections, word reveals become instant text, and the orb's drift freezes or slows. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3 asks that interaction-triggered motion can be disabled, and SC 2.2.2 covers anything that moves on its own for more than five seconds ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). **Accessibility is the weakest sub-score among 2026 Awwwards AI winners** (6.20–6.80 on Dev Award breakdowns for Fourmula AI and Heron AI). Shipping full reduced-motion and keyboard parity on the dock is therefore a cheap way to beat the reference set rather than merely match it ([Awwwards: Fourmula AI](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/fourmula-ai); [Awwwards: Heron AI](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/heron-ai)).

## Conclusion

Having no customers looks like Amariya's weakness, but this research suggests it can be turned into a strength. The established players are demo-gated, price-hidden and guarantee-free, so the most persuasive thing a new entrant can do is **let the buyer verify everything without trusting anyone**: dial the agent, read the price, read the refund terms, watch the seat count move, and see the seven days laid out. Each of those is a claim the visitor can check. Each also creates a legal and operational obligation: a real seat database, working demo lines in three niches, an unambiguous guarantee, and BAA coverage confirmed through ElevenLabs and any LLM sub-processor. The persuasion and the compliance are the same work.

What remains uncertain matters for sequencing. No conversion data exists for any of these page patterns in this category. Buyer response to one-time versus monthly pricing is untested. The guarantee wording conflict ("7-day refund" versus "live in 7 days or your money back") is unresolved. So the page should launch as a hypothesis and be measured on applications that become booked calls, not raw form submissions. It should also be judged on whether the "one-time" price draws buyers who will stay or bargain hunters, because the lifetime definition and the 500-minute overage terms will decide whether the founding cohort becomes the case studies the second version of this page needs.
